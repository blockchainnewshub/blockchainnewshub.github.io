Binance traces record $6.34B theft to staged employee compromise

Binance says a staged employee compromise enabled the $6.34 billion theft, now the largest reported crypto heist, while recovery and independent review remain incomplete.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 4, 2026 · 08:55 UTC#628a334 min read

Binance says attackers stole about $6.34 billion in digital assets on Oct. 29 after building access through a staged employee compromise, a loss far above the $1.5 billion Bybit theft that had set the previous crypto-heist record. In its Nov. 3 initial findings and response update, the exchange described a campaign that moved from malware on an engineer’s work computer to unauthorized transactions from its hot wallets. The account is Binance’s own preliminary investigation; the company says an independent review is still to come.

How did the attackers reach Binance’s wallets?

Binance says the attackers used a fake recruitment process to compromise an engineer, then used an internal AI tool and stolen credentials to reach wallet infrastructure. The exchange’s timeline says an attacker accessed an engineer’s computer on Oct. 29, deployed a container and ran a script that reached the wallet signing service. That service authorizes transactions; the breach therefore bypassed the normal withdrawal process without, according to Binance, requiring a leak of a wallet’s private key.

Between 20:05 and 20:09 UTC, five Binance hot wallets sent 233.46 million XRP to an attacker-controlled address. Binance also reported transfers of about 970,500 SOL and 44.2 million OP, followed by 18,270 BTC. The attackers then exploited Ethereum’s EIP-7702 authorization mechanism, which lets an account delegate some execution rights to a smart contract. Binance says the resulting permissions let the attackers continue moving assets even after the exchange suspended withdrawals.

That distinction shaped the response. Shutting down ordinary withdrawals could restrict normal outflows, but it did not automatically erase permissions already established on-chain. Binance says its hot-wallet system used multi-party computation, with no single private key available to revoke those permissions immediately, so containment required additional transactions. The final unauthorized transfer it reported was 2,961.9 ETH, finalized at 23:56 UTC.

How does the loss compare with earlier crypto thefts?

The scale is exceptional even against recent exchange breaches. The Associated Press reported the $1.5 billion Bybit theft as the previous comparison; Binance’s estimated impact is more than four times that amount. AP also reported that blockchain firms attributed the Binance attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking unit, while Binance’s Nov. 3 update says the latest FBI information identifies North Korea as responsible. The attribution is not the same as a public identification of individual attackers.

Binance’s update put the amount frozen or intercepted at about $2.85 billion, subject to reconciliation and price changes. That is a substantial recovery effort, but freezing assets and replacing the value of stolen assets are separate measures. Binance said it had set aside more than the amount needed to cover the loss and that 98% of impacted token assets had been replenished and were verifiable in its Proof of Reserves as of Oct. 31. It said it was still purchasing the remaining, less-liquid assets.

What will show whether the response is enough?

For customers, Binance’s commitment to absorb the loss limits the direct financial impact the exchange says they will bear. For the wider market, the incident shows the trade-off in hot-wallet operations: keeping assets available for withdrawals supports exchange activity, while signing systems connected to that process create a target whose compromise can outlast a withdrawal halt. Binance says it has rebuilt affected systems, replaced devices, revoked related credentials and tightened wallet controls. It has also engaged outside cybersecurity experts to review its findings.

The investigation remains open, and Binance says some security details will stay confidential. The next signals are the independent review’s conclusions, whether the full value of affected assets is replenished, and whether investigators or blockchain firms confirm how much of the stolen crypto can ultimately be recovered.
