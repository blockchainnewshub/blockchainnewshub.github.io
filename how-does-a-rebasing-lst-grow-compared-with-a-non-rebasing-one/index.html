How Does a Rebasing LST Grow Compared With a Non-Rebasing One?

A rebasing token in liquid staking adds tokens as rewards arrive; a non-rebasing token keeps your count fixed while each token gains ETH value.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 20, 2026 · 17:34 UTC#2c95204 min read

A rebasing liquid staking token credits staking rewards by increasing the number of tokens in your wallet; a non-rebasing one keeps your count steady and increases the ETH value per token. That is the key difference behind the search for what a rebasing token in liquid staking means in practice.

Both formats pass on staking rewards. They differ in how they account for your share of those rewards, which can affect wallet displays, DeFi integrations and the way you track a position.

The rewards are similar; the balance changes differently

A rebasing token updates your token count as your share of staking rewards accrues. Lido’s stETH balance rebases daily with the oracle report: after a report, your wallet can show a slightly larger balance, reflecting rewards since the previous update. A rebase can also be negative if penalties outweigh rewards, so the displayed balance can fall.

Take a holder with 10 stETH. After a positive daily rebase, the balance becomes slightly more than 10 stETH. The increase is visible as more tokens, rather than a separate reward payment. The exact change depends on the rewards and any penalties reflected in the report.

With a non-rebasing token, the token count stays put while its exchange rate against ETH rises as rewards accrue. Ten wstETH remains 10 wstETH, but it can redeem for more stETH over time. Rocket Pool’s rETH uses the same non-rebasing approach: its ETH value grows through an exchange rate instead of a growing wallet balance.

The distinction is accounting, not a different source of staking yield. A rebasing balance makes the reward visible in token units; a non-rebasing balance puts the change into the value represented by each token. Comparing token counts alone can therefore make the formats look more different than their underlying reward accounting is.

Wallet visibility trades off against DeFi convenience

Rebasing makes earned yield easy to spot in a wallet because the displayed stETH amount rises. That convenience can create friction in DeFi: a protocol or bridge that caches balances may not account correctly for a balance that changes through rebases. Many DeFi protocols and L2 bridges use wstETH because its token count stays fixed, which is simpler for contracts to integrate and track.

There is a small but practical wrinkle when moving stETH. The balance is represented through shares, and transfers round in shares; transferring what looks like your full balance can leave 1–2 wei behind. That remainder is tiny, but it matters if you need an exact transfer or want to empty a position completely. Use wstETH where a fixed token amount helps, or transfer by shares when the interface supports it and exact amounts matter.

The same accounting distinction matters at eigen layer: LST deposits are recorded as strategy shares, so a rebasing deposit’s growth appears in the value of each share rather than in the share count. That leaves a trade-off for an experienced holder. stETH shows rewards directly in the wallet, but its changing balance can complicate exact transfers and integrations. wstETH and rETH keep the count stable, which eases tracking in many contracts, while requiring you to follow the exchange rate to see how much ETH the position represents. The more your position moves through DeFi or restaking contracts, the more the fixed count may matter; if you mainly want a visible balance change, rebasing is more direct.

Related

Is wstETH better than stETH?

wstETH is a better fit when you value a stable token count for DeFi use, transfers or tracking. stETH is more direct when you want staking rewards reflected as a changing wallet balance. Neither format changes the basic fact that rewards pass through the token’s accounting; the practical choice depends on which display and integration behavior suits your use.

How do you convert stETH to wstETH?

You can wrap stETH to receive wstETH, converting a rebasing balance into a non-rebasing token whose ETH value changes through its exchange rate. When planning the conversion, check the amount the interface will wrap and the resulting wstETH, especially if you are trying to move an exact balance. The fixed count can make later DeFi interactions easier to track, though you will need to look at the exchange rate to follow the position’s value.

Choose rebasing when seeing rewards added to your wallet matters most; choose non-rebasing when predictable token counts reduce friction in DeFi and restaking. The deciding reason is how your position will be used, not a difference in how the two formats pass on staking rewards.
