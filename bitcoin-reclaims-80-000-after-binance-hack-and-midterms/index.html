Bitcoin reclaims $80,000 after Binance hack and midterms

Bitcoin rose 3.5% to $80,500 on Wednesday after a post-hack slide, while traders weighed the Binance recovery, election outcome and broader market risks.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 6, 2026 · 19:10 UTC#f0446f3 min read

Bitcoin climbed back above $80,000 on Wednesday, rising 3.5% to $80,500 after losses following the $6.3 billion Binance breach and early volatility around the U.S. midterm results. The move recovered much of the roughly 4% post-hack decline, but left the price below the $80,000–$82,000 range analysts say it needs to sustain. The Block’s market report said Ether gained nearly 6% to $2,433, while Solana and XRP rose 2.5%.

Did the election result change the market’s direction?

U.S. futures initially fell as much as 2.5% on early counts before recovering, according to Nexo analysts cited by The Block. Democrats regained control of both chambers of Congress, but Nexo said the recovery suggested investors saw the result as adding clarity rather than changing the growth outlook. The sequence is consistent with a muted market response; it does not establish that the election caused Bitcoin’s rebound.

Crypto trading data also pointed more to repositioning than a rush into leveraged bets. Futures volume rose 27%, while open interest increased 3%; Bitcoin open interest grew more slowly than its price. Around $469 million in leveraged positions were liquidated. Those figures show traders resetting exposure as prices rose, though they do not reveal whether the move will attract sustained new demand.

The broader crypto market joined the advance. Coinbase and Strategy each gained about 6%, while Block rose 15% after its earnings report and Strive climbed roughly 12%. The Official Trump memecoin moved in the opposite direction, falling 17%. The split shows that Bitcoin’s recovery was part of a wider rally in some crypto-linked assets, but not a uniform rise across the sector.

What did the Binance breach put at risk?

The theft tested confidence in exchange custody, rather than exposing a flaw in Bitcoin itself. The FBI attributed the theft to North Korean actors known as TraderTraitor and said they were converting assets primarily into Ethereum, according to the Associated Press. Bitwise chief investment officer Matt Hougan said Bitcoin’s roughly 4% decline was broadly in line with other risk assets; he argued that the breach involved exchange hot wallets, not the Bitcoin network.

That distinction matters for the alternatives available to holders. Bitwise pointed to institutional custodians, cold storage and spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds as options that keep underlying assets offline or add withdrawal controls. These arrangements may reduce exposure to an exchange hot-wallet breach, but do not remove market-price risk. Binance, meanwhile, said it would cover the stolen funds itself.

In its November 4 recovery update, Binance said it had replenished about 98% of the impacted amount and was buying the remaining, less-liquid tokens through market and over-the-counter channels. The exchange also reported that a new proof-of-reserves snapshot covered 44 tokens, up from 10, with its full zero-knowledge proof still being generated. These are company-reported recovery figures, not an independent assessment of the incident.

What could confirm that Bitcoin’s rebound is holding?

Analysts cited by The Block are watching whether Bitcoin holds $76,000–$78,000 and sustains its return above $80,000–$82,000. They are also monitoring Binance’s withdrawal process and reserve reporting, credit spreads, oil prices, long-term Treasury yields and whether spot ETF inflows return. Those signals will help distinguish a lasting recovery from a brief bounce after liquidations and election-related volatility.
