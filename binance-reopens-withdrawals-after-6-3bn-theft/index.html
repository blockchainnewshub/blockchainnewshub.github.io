Binance Reopens Withdrawals After $6.3bn Theft

Binance has begun restoring withdrawals in phases after a $6.3bn hot-wallet theft, with most affected assets replenished and the attack still under investigation.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 2, 2026 · 11:31 UTC#7587c03 min read

Binance began restoring withdrawals in phases on November 1 after attackers stole about $6.3 billion in token assets from its hot wallets, a breach the exchange says it will cover. The theft is larger than the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in 2025, which had been crypto’s previous biggest recorded exchange theft, according to the Associated Press’s account of the Binance hack. The incident interrupted customers’ access to withdrawals; Binance said deposits remained open, and its updates describe a pause on withdrawals rather than trading.

The scale puts pressure on a familiar exchange response: freeze movement while investigating, then use company resources to reassure customers that balances remain covered. Binance says it has set aside more than $6.34 billion for the losses, including funds from its Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU) and corporate treasury. That promise addresses the immediate question of who bears the loss, but it does not settle how the breach happened or how quickly users can move every asset off-platform.

What did the attackers access, and what remains unknown?

Binance says its hot wallets were compromised at about 20:05 UTC on October 29, producing unauthorized withdrawals. Hot wallets are connected to systems that process transactions, unlike cold wallets kept offline. Binance’s initial investigation pointed to a potentially compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop and access to a service container, but the exchange later said it had found additional points of breach. It reported no evidence that wallet private keys had leaked.

The company has not yet published a full account of the attack path. That leaves an important distinction between what is known and what is still under investigation: Binance has confirmed the loss and unauthorized withdrawals, but has not established publicly how the attackers gained and retained access, or which controls failed. It says a post-incident report will follow after the investigation is complete. The gap matters because the proposed laptop entry point alone may not explain the additional breach points the exchange says it found.

How much has Binance recovered, and what did the pause cost users?

Binance said on November 1 that it had replenished about 95% of the impacted amount, up from 80% in its October 30 update. It said the remaining 5% involved less-liquid tokens outside its top ten affected assets, which could take longer to source through market and over-the-counter purchases. The exchange also said roughly $3 billion of affected assets had been frozen on-chain. Those frozen funds are not the same as recovered funds: freezing can restrict movement, while recovery depends on whether the assets can be returned.

The withdrawal pause gave Binance time to check affected systems and reopen access gradually, but it also left customers unable to move funds during the checks. In its withdrawal recovery update, Binance said the first phase would restore selected assets across networks including Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB Smart Chain, Solana and XRP, with more added after readiness checks. The phased approach limits the number of services returned at once; it also means access depends on the asset and network a customer uses.

What should users watch next?

The next tests are whether Binance completes the rollout across the remaining networks, closes the 5% replenishment gap and publishes evidence customers can independently check. Binance says its October 31 proof-of-reserves snapshot covers several major assets at 100%, with the full zero-knowledge proof expected by November 3. That can help verify backing for listed balances, while the promised incident report should explain the security failure. Until both arrive, the recovery figures and the attack’s mechanism remain claims from an exchange whose investigation is ongoing.
