HSBC brings HKD stablecoin payments to PayMe users

HSBC has launched its HKD stablecoin on PayMe, opening in-app transfers and merchant payments to millions as Hong Kong’s licensed market takes shape.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 25, 2026 · 16:04 UTC#0eb4273 min read

HSBC began rolling out RedCoin, its first Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, on PayMe on 23 October, putting a bank-issued token within an app used by more than 3.3 million people. The bank’s launch announcement says users will be able to activate wallets in the coming days and use them for peer-to-peer transfers and payments at more than 100,000 merchants. That moves the project from April’s licensing and launch plans into retail use, while keeping the first stage inside PayMe.

The distinction is access: HSBC is starting with everyday transfers and purchases, while Hong Kong’s other first licensed issuer, Anchorpoint Financial, has begun a phased rollout of its HKDAP stablecoin aimed initially at institutions and professional investors, according to The Block’s report. RedCoin therefore reaches a broader retail audience at launch, though its use is limited to PayMe for now.

What can PayMe users do with RedCoin now?

They can use it for peer-to-peer payments and merchant purchases through a stablecoin wallet in the app. HSBC says the wallet activation will open over the coming days; the launch announcement does not say that all PayMe users can activate it immediately. The South China Morning Post reports that users can activate without a minimum balance or additional account-opening requirements, and that the interface is designed to remain close to the existing PayMe experience.

That setup trades a standalone crypto-wallet experience for access through a familiar payment app. It may lower the steps needed to try a stablecoin, while leaving RedCoin’s initial reach dependent on PayMe and its payment network. HSBC says the coin is Hong Kong dollar-denominated and is subject to the Hong Kong Monetary Authority’s issuer licence and the financial-crime controls required under the Stablecoins Ordinance.

How does this compare with the earlier plan?

In April, HSBC said it planned an HKD stablecoin for the second half of 2026, integrated with PayMe and its Hong Kong mobile banking app. The October launch delivers the PayMe payment phase first; access through HSBC’s banking app and subscriptions to tokenised investment products remain planned for later this year. HSBC says those products will include a share class of its HKD money market fund.

The phased sequence gives the bank room to establish transfers and merchant payments before adding investment functions. The trade-off is that RedCoin’s reach and uses remain narrower than HSBC’s longer-term plan until the additional app and products arrive. A staff pilot involving nearly 100 users tested minting, wallet activation and live transfers, according to HSBC, but that is not evidence yet of broad customer uptake.

What should readers watch next?

The immediate signals are whether wallet activation reaches PayMe users as scheduled, how often customers use RedCoin at merchants, and whether transfers work reliably at scale. HSBC has said it intends to add wallet functionality to its Hong Kong mobile banking app later this year and expand into wholesale corporate and institutional uses. Those steps would broaden the token beyond PayMe’s retail payments; until then, adoption will be measured mainly by use inside the existing app.
