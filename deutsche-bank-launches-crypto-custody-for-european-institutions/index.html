Deutsche Bank launches crypto custody for European institutions

Deutsche Bank has opened crypto custody to European institutions and agreed to explore tokenized products with Deutsche Asset Management, moving from plan to service.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 12, 2026 · 06:48 UTC#1379244 min read

Deutsche Bank has launched digital asset custody for institutional and corporate clients in Europe, turning a September plan that was still awaiting regulatory approval into a live service. The bank says it received approval from Germany’s BaFin; according to Deutsche Bank’s launch announcement, the initial offering covers Bitcoin, Ether and selected stablecoins or e-money tokens, including USDC, EURC and EURAU. The shift puts a major European bank into a market where clients can also use specialist custodians or manage wallets themselves. Deutsche Bank offers a regulated banking counterparty and key management; clients give up direct control of those keys.

The launch came alongside a memorandum of understanding with Deutsche Asset Management, the group’s majority-owned asset manager, to explore tokenized financial products. The agreement broadens the move from safekeeping crypto assets toward services around funds and settlement, though it remains exploratory. The Block’s report says the work could cover tokenized money market funds, crypto-asset exchange-traded commodities (ETCs) and stablecoins.

What can clients do with the service now?

Initially, eligible clients of Deutsche Bank’s Corporate Bank and Investment Bank can hold selected digital assets with the bank and transfer them to other parties. The custody service manages wallets and private keys on clients’ behalf, so institutions do not need to build and operate their own key-storage systems. Deutsche Bank describes a layered setup: dedicated hardware protects key material, responsibilities are split among staff, and more than one person must approve important actions. It also uses offline cold storage and backup and recovery arrangements.

That model shifts key-management work to a bank with established custody operations, while adding the bank’s controls and processes between a client and its assets. Specialist providers can offer digital-asset infrastructure, while self-custody leaves key control with the owner. Each approach allocates operational responsibility differently. The bank’s announcement says tokenized financial instruments may be added later; other assets depend on client demand and the bank’s product approval, risk management and regulatory processes. The initial scope is therefore a defined set of assets, rather than an open-ended custody service.

What does the Deutsche Asset Management agreement commit to?

The memorandum commits the two firms to explore commercial and technical collaboration, rather than confirming that a tokenized fund or stablecoin is ready to launch. The Block reports that discussions include custody and settlement, liquidity management, treasury and distribution using Deutsche Bank’s platform. Deutsche Asset Management chief product officer Manfred Bauer said the firm planned to launch a tokenized money market fund. Deutsche Asset Management reported €1.22 trillion in assets under management as of September 30, according to The Block. That scale gives the proposed work a substantial traditional asset-management base, but it does not establish how much investor demand a specific tokenized product would attract.

For Deutsche Bank, the combined move connects custody with a possible pipeline of products that may need custody, settlement and liquidity services. It also extends the bank’s 2023 partnership with Taurus to develop digital asset custody and tokenization services, according to The Block. The bank’s stated longer-term aim is an integrated regulated ecosystem for custody, tokenization and settlement. The trade-off is clear: institutional clients gain a bank-run route into selected digital assets, while access depends on the bank’s approved product list and controls.

Which signals will show whether the launch expands?

The next evidence will be the first products made available, any expansion beyond the initial European client base, and whether the memorandum produces a launched tokenized money market fund or other services. Deutsche Bank has said it may add assets subject to demand and internal review. Until those steps are announced, the live custody service is the concrete change; the asset-management collaboration remains a plan to explore.
