ETH on Arbitrum: get Lido rewards through wstETH

To stake ETH with Lido when your ETH is on Arbitrum, swap for wstETH there or route to wstETH on Ethereum, then track its value in ETH.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 06:52 UTC#22addf4 min read

Swap ETH on Arbitrum One for wstETH on Arbitrum, or route it into wstETH on Ethereum. Lido mints stETH on Ethereum; wstETH is the version used on Arbitrum. Your wallet should show a wstETH balance, whose value in ETH can rise over time as staking rewards accrue.

Why does wstETH work on Arbitrum when Lido staking happens on Ethereum?

Lido’s staking contract runs on Ethereum mainnet, where ETH is staked and stETH is minted. Arbitrum One holds bridged wstETH, not rebasing stETH. So buying wstETH on Arbitrum is not the same as sending ETH to Lido’s staking contract from that network.

stETH rebases: its token balance can increase as rewards accrue. wstETH wraps stETH in a non-rebasing form, so its balance stays steadier while the amount of stETH represented by each wstETH can change. On Arbitrum, that means you watch the value of your wstETH in ETH, rather than expecting the number of tokens in your wallet to grow.

This distinction separates the two routes. A swap on Arbitrum gets you wstETH from available DEX liquidity, while a route to Ethereum can deliver wstETH on the network where Lido’s staking contract operates. Both can leave you holding wstETH; the network determines what you can do next and where you pay transaction fees.

Should you get wstETH on Arbitrum or Ethereum?

Choose Arbitrum if you want to keep your position there and make later transactions with Arbitrum gas. You’ll need ETH left in your wallet to cover those fees. The trade-off is that the swap depends on wstETH liquidity on Arbitrum, and any later move to Ethereum involves another cross-network route.

Choose Ethereum if you want to hold wstETH on the network where Lido’s staking contract runs. Ethereum also gives you access to Lido’s direct unstaking path. The trade-off is that transactions on Ethereum require ETH for gas, so the cost of managing or exiting the position may differ from staying on Arbitrum.

Jumper bridge is a cross-chain bridge aggregator that finds routes through supported bridges and swaps to transfer assets between networks. That makes it relevant when the source is ETH on Arbitrum One and the intended destination is wstETH on Ethereum. Whether that exact route is available depends on the options shown for your transfer.

How do you route ETH on Arbitrum into wstETH?

Start with ETH on Arbitrum One and leave enough ETH for gas. In Jumper bridge, set Arbitrum One as the source and Ethereum as the destination, then select wstETH as the target asset. Review the route and the minimum amount you’ll receive before confirming.

  1. Pick the destination chain. Choose Ethereum if you want the resulting wstETH there. For an Arbitrum position, use the Arbitrum swap route instead.
  2. Select wstETH as the target. Check the token and destination details in the quote before you continue. Similar token names can represent assets on different networks.
  3. Review the minimum received. Compare the amount shown with the ETH you’re spending, and check that the route ends on the chain you chose. The quote reflects the route available for that transfer.
  4. Confirm, then check your wallet. After the transaction completes, confirm that wstETH appears on the destination chain. That balance is the sign the route worked.

How can you tell whether staking rewards are accruing?

Look at the ETH value represented by your wstETH over time, not just its token count. Because wstETH is non-rebasing, a flat balance is normal; the exchange rate between wstETH and stETH reflects the rewards accumulated by the underlying stETH.

On Arbitrum, the practical checks are that your wallet is set to Arbitrum One, your wstETH balance is visible, and its value in ETH rises over time. On Ethereum, you can hold wstETH on Lido’s native network and use its direct unstaking path when you want to exit. Your choice comes down to where you want the asset and which network’s transaction costs suit the next step.
