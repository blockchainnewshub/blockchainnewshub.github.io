Mesh links wallet balances to Visa’s 175 million merchants

Mesh says participating wallet users will be able to pay from crypto or stablecoin balances at Visa merchants without cashing out or carrying a separate card.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 20, 2026 · 18:11 UTC#6609f93 min read

Mesh and Visa announced a partnership on Oct. 19 to let users pay at Visa merchants from crypto, stablecoin or fiat balances held in participating wallet apps. The shift is from cashing out to a bank account or using a platform-linked card to a wallet-native checkout: a Visa credential is provisioned behind the scenes, while Mesh handles funding and conversion. The network covers more than 175 million merchant locations in over 200 countries and territories, according to Mesh’s partnership announcement.

That changes the route into the existing card network, rather than the merchant’s checkout. Users would not have to sell, transfer or preload funds before paying, and merchants would receive a standard payment without new hardware or workflow changes. The scale is Visa’s: the company reported $14.2 trillion in payments volume in fiscal 2025. Whether the new route reaches ordinary users at that scale depends on which wallets join and when; neither has been announced.

How will wallet balances become a Visa payment?

Mesh says it will orchestrate how a transaction is funded and converted across supported crypto, stablecoin and fiat balances. A participating platform connects through Mesh instead of building and operating its own card program. At checkout, the user pays in-store or online through the wallet app; Visa’s credential sits behind that experience, and the merchant sees a regular Visa payment.

The arrangement shifts the work from the user to the infrastructure. Cashing out first requires moving funds into a bank account; a platform card ties spending to a separate card program. Mesh’s approach aims to remove those steps while preserving Visa acceptance at the point of sale. It does not remove conversion: Mesh says it handles conversion at payment time. The announcement does not specify supported assets, conversion rates, fees or how users will choose which balance funds a purchase.

What does it change compared with crypto cards?

Visa-linked crypto cards already connect digital assets to merchant payments, but users still need a card issued through a particular platform. For example, a 2025 Visa and Bridge product let developers offer stablecoin-backed Visa cards, with Bridge handling the backend and currency conversions, as The Block reported. Mesh’s proposal instead lets a wallet or platform add spending without running its own card program.

The trade-off is dependence on participating platforms and Mesh’s transaction orchestration. Mesh says its network connects hundreds of wallets, exchanges and platforms, and that Visa access can come through a single integration. But the announcement names no launch partners for this service. It also says Mesh will act as program manager, handling user onboarding and know-your-customer checks, so the exact sign-up and account requirements remain unclear.

When can users try the service?

There is no launch date yet. Mesh says initial availability, supported devices and participating platforms will be announced as rollout details are finalized. Until then, the partnership describes a planned payment route rather than a service users can rely on today. The next signals are the first named wallets, the markets and devices they support, and whether the balance and conversion terms are disclosed before launch.
