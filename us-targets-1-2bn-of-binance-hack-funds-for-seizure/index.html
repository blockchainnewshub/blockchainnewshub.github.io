US targets $1.2bn of Binance hack funds for seizure

The US has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2bn of the crypto stolen from Binance, while over $2.7bn has been frozen so far.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 11:59 UTC#cb90a33 min read

The US has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step beyond the broader freeze of more than $2.7 billion in assets traced since the theft. The Justice Department’s announcement on the Binance funds says the department obtained that authority as part of an ongoing effort to stop the assets moving beyond law enforcement’s reach. The distinction matters: a freeze blocks movement, while a seizure or restraint backed by a court order advances the government’s legal control over specified funds; it does not by itself establish that the money has been returned to victims.

The theft happened on or about October 29. The Justice Department says North Korean military members, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from the exchange. The Associated Press reports the FBI’s seizure announcement and describes the theft as about $6.3 billion. Against that scale, the court-authorized amount is substantial but covers only part of the stolen assets: US officials and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total, according to the department.

From a broad freeze to court-backed action

The recovery effort combines government investigation with cooperation from virtual asset service providers, or VASPs. The department says agencies worked with these providers to interdict the funds, while the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating recovery work. That route can interrupt transfers across crypto services, but it depends on tracing assets and getting providers to act quickly. Court authorization adds a legal process to the intervention; a freeze alone does not settle who ultimately has a valid claim to the funds.

The two figures describe different stages and should not be added together: the more than $1.2 billion is part of the more than $2.7 billion already frozen, not an additional recovery. Nor does the announcement say the full $1.2 billion has been forfeited or returned. In typical cases, the government files a forfeiture claim and a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to the property, AP reports. The public announcement does not identify the tokens or wallets covered by the court authority, or state how much has reached claimants.

The alternative to this coordinated approach would be to rely on asset holders or exchanges to block suspicious transfers without the same court-backed step. The government’s account credits both public and private action with freezing the larger sum, and says authorities have now secured permission to seize or restrain a portion of it. That can make movement harder for the attackers, while leaving ownership and final distribution unresolved. The Justice Department says the actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, which keeps tracing and coordination central to any further recovery.

Tracing and forfeiture remain open

The FBI had issued a cyber alert the previous week, asking VASPs to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses used by TraderTraitor to launder the Binance assets. The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds are ongoing. The immediate signals to watch are whether the government identifies additional restrained assets, whether providers block further transfers from the flagged addresses, and whether forfeiture proceedings establish a path for returning funds. Until then, the confirmed result is court authority over more than $1.2 billion within a larger pool of frozen assets—not a completed recovery of the Binance loss.
