What Does Last Updated Mean on a Token Approval? Date Source

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 22:41 UTC#c942944 min read

The Last Updated date on a revokecash approval row is the date of the block where that approval was most recently set or changed for the token and spender. It comes from the timestamp of the latest approval event, so it tells you when the current allowance or operator approval was recorded on-chain.

What does the last updated date mean on an approval?

The date records an approval change, not when you last used the token or spent the allowance. For an ERC-20 token, calling approve emits an Approval event that sets the amount a spender can use. For an ERC-721 collection, setApprovalForAll emits an ApprovalForAll event that grants or removes an operator’s authority over the collection’s tokens.

Each event is included in a block, and that block has a timestamp. The approval row uses the timestamp of the latest relevant event for that token and spender. A later approval can therefore replace an older date, even when the spender stays the same.

When triaging rows, consider the date alongside the token, spender, and allowance details. That is the approval information revokecash presents; what Revoke.cash shows for each approval explains how to read those fields together.

Say you approved a DEX router in 2023 and re-approved that same router last week. The row’s Last Updated date will show last week, because the latest approval event was recorded then. A recent date on a familiar DEX can mean you renewed its approval, rather than that a new spender appeared. Confirm the spender address before deciding whether the row is expected.

An old date also needs context. It tells you when the approval was last set or changed; it does not establish whether the approval is still needed, whether you still use the app, or whether the spender remains one you trust. Treating an old date as proof that an approval is harmless can leave an allowance or operator permission in place longer than you intend.

To verify a date you do not recognize, open the transaction on Etherscan and inspect its details. The transaction can help confirm which address submitted it and which contract was involved. Compare those addresses with the spender shown in the approval row; a familiar app name alone may not tell you which contract received permission.

These dates help you sort approvals by when they were last changed, while the spender and permission determine what each row represents. Use the event date as a clue for your review, then decide whether the permission is still appropriate.

Related questions

Why does an approval I never remember show a recent date?

A recent date can appear because a wallet, app, or transaction flow submitted an approval you do not recall making. It may also reflect re-approving a familiar spender, which resets the date even when the spender has not changed. Open the linked transaction on Etherscan to review its sender, contract, and approval event before deciding whether it is expected.

Is Last Updated shown in my local time?

The displayed time depends on how the approval page formats the block timestamp, while the underlying timestamp belongs to the on-chain block. If the time seems different from your records, compare the date and time zone shown by the page with the timestamp on Etherscan.

What should I check next?

Can I see when an approval was last spent?

Last Updated records the latest approval change, not the latest time the spender used that permission. Token transfers or other on-chain activity may provide clues about use, but they are separate from the approval event that sets the date.

Does revoking show up as a new Last Updated date?

Revoking an ERC-20 allowance or an ERC-721 operator approval changes the permission, so the revocation can be recorded as a newer approval event and update the row’s date. The permission’s current status matters too: check that it is revoked after the transaction is confirmed.
