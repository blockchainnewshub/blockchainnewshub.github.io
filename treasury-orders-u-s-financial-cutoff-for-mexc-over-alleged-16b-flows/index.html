Treasury orders U.S. financial cutoff for MEXC over alleged $16B flows

FinCEN ordered U.S. firms to block transfers involving MEXC over alleged $16 billion in illicit flows, putting its U.S. financial access at stake once effective.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 10, 2026 · 10:52 UTC#b49fc23 min read

The U.S. Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) ordered a cutoff of U.S. financial transfers involving MEXC Global, citing at least $16 billion in alleged illicit transactions over five years. The Treasury announcement says the action targets funds moving to or from the exchange through U.S. financial institutions; it does not say MEXC’s global platform is being shut down. That distinction makes the order narrower than a full exchange ban, while putting a major U.S. financial connection at risk.

The order, issued November 6 under Section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act, takes effect 30 days after publication in the Federal Register. Until then, the date of publication will determine when the cutoff begins. According to CoinDesk’s report, MEXC said it was operating normally while reviewing the order and had launched an independent review of its anti-money laundering and sanctions controls.

Why does an Iran-focused campaign rely on a Russia law?

Treasury announced the move as part of Operation Economic Outcast, its campaign to cut Iran off from global finance. But the legal basis, Section 9714, concerns Russian illicit finance. CoinDesk reported that the provision can be used against foreign institutions tied to Russian illicit finance, and that the order rests on MEXC’s alleged Russian dealings. The headline figure of $16 billion covers transactions Treasury connected to several categories of alleged illicit activity; the Russian-linked amount it specified was nearly $1.5 billion between May 2021 and May 2026.

Of that Russian-linked total, Treasury said $232 million involved direct transactions with entities sanctioned by the U.S., EU or U.K. It also cited at least $26 million in transactions benefiting Russia-based A7 and nearly $200 million connected to A7A5, a stablecoin Treasury says the network used to help clients evade sanctions. The department said MEXC processed transactions with sanctioned exchange Garantex and its successor, Grinex, as recently as June 2026.

How much of MEXC’s business does the order reach?

The order covers U.S. financial institutions’ transmission of funds to or from MEXC. It also preserves their existing Bank Secrecy Act duties, including suspicious activity reporting, and does not change OFAC sanctions obligations. Treasury warns that violations may bring civil or criminal penalties. The text therefore puts pressure on U.S.-connected payment and transfer channels without, by its terms, blocking every customer or crypto transaction worldwide.

Treasury described MEXC as one of the world’s 20 largest crypto exchanges, with more than $1 billion in daily transactions and about $5 billion in reserves as of September 2026. CoinDesk reported it was the largest crypto exchange targeted under Section 9714; earlier exchange targets under the provision included Bitzlato in 2023 and PM2BTC in 2024. Compared with those precedents, the scale of MEXC raises the stakes of enforcement, but the specific cutoff remains tied to U.S. financial institutions.

What will show whether the cutoff bites?

The first signal is the Federal Register publication date, which starts the 30-day clock. After that, watch for evidence of changes to MEXC’s U.S.-linked transfers, any public update from the exchange on its controls, and further enforcement activity. Treasury’s figures are allegations supporting an administrative order, not findings from a court judgment. The practical test will be whether the measure constrains the financial routes it covers while MEXC continues to serve users through other channels.
