Mantle Bridge gas: what to hold on each chain

Mantle uses MNT for gas while Ethereum uses ETH, so bridging an asset does not always leave you ready to transact; budget for both chains and the withdrawal claim.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 23, 2026 · 01:01 UTC#d367c04 min read

To use Mantle Bridge, you need ETH for transactions on Ethereum and MNT for transactions on Mantle; the token you bridge is not automatically the token that pays gas. That differs from many Ethereum layer-2 networks, where ETH pays fees on both Ethereum and the destination network. The practical trade-off is straightforward: Mantle transactions use MNT, but a bridge transfer can leave your wallet with the asset you wanted and no MNT to move it.

Each network charges for work done on that network. An Ethereum deposit may involve an ERC-20 approval and a separate deposit transaction, both paid in ETH. After the asset arrives on Mantle, sending, swapping or withdrawing it requires MNT. For a fuller comparison of route choices and costs, see Mantle Bridge. A bridge moves assets between networks; it does not make their fee currencies interchangeable.

Which token pays gas on Ethereum and Mantle?

ETH pays gas on Ethereum, while MNT pays gas on Mantle. Treat those as separate balances in your wallet, even when the same wallet address is connected to both networks. A balance shown on one network cannot pay for a transaction on the other.

For an Ethereum-to-Mantle deposit, keep ETH on Ethereum to cover the approval, if required, and the deposit itself. If the destination asset is USDC, for example, the USDC balance can arrive without providing any MNT. You will need MNT before you can send that USDC to another address, trade it on Mantle, or start a withdrawal.

For a Mantle-to-Ethereum withdrawal, keep MNT on Mantle to initiate the withdrawal. The process may also require a later claim transaction on Ethereum, which needs ETH in the wallet making that claim. The funds waiting to be claimed do not pay its gas. In practice, this means planning for the start and finish of the transfer, not only the transaction that moves the asset across.

What should you hold before bridging?

Before confirming, check which network holds your funds and which wallet will sign each step. A useful minimum plan is to reserve the relevant gas token on each side and avoid sending your entire fee balance into the bridge.

  • Depositing from Ethereum: keep ETH on Ethereum for any approval and the deposit. Consider how you will obtain MNT on Mantle after the transfer.
  • Using assets on Mantle: keep some MNT there for sends, swaps, approvals and other transactions. USDC, USDT or bridged ETH cannot pay ordinary Mantle gas.
  • Withdrawing to Ethereum: keep MNT on Mantle for the withdrawal and ETH on Ethereum for the claim when it becomes available.
  • Bridging MNT itself: check the receiving network and resulting balance. A token balance on Ethereum is not the same as MNT available for gas on Mantle.

The exact amount to reserve depends on the transaction and current network conditions, so review the wallet’s fee estimate before signing. If you are depositing a token that needs approval, account for that separate transaction rather than assuming the displayed bridge fee covers it.

Can another route solve the gas-token problem?

Some third-party bridge or swap routes offer a destination gas top-up, or let you receive a different asset as part of the transfer. These can reduce the need to arrange MNT separately, but they add route details to compare: the quoted output, any service or swap costs, and the contracts and providers involved. A direct bridge is simpler when you already have gas on both networks; a route with a top-up may be easier when you do not, if its total cost and terms are clear.

Do not assume that choosing MNT as the asset to transfer pays every fee along the way. The source transaction still needs the source network’s gas token, and a destination top-up only helps if it delivers usable MNT to the wallet that will transact. Confirm the network, recipient address, asset and displayed fees before signing. After a transfer, check the destination balance on the correct network rather than relying only on a matching token symbol.

The useful rule is to plan gas by transaction location: ETH for Ethereum approvals, deposits and claims; MNT for activity on Mantle. Watch the wallet’s fee estimate, the bridge’s withdrawal and claim status, and whether the chosen route actually credits MNT on arrival. Those details determine whether the asset is merely across the bridge or ready to use.
