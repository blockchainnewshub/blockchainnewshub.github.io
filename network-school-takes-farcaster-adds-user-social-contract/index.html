Network School takes Farcaster, adds user social contract

Network School has taken Farcaster from Neynar and will condition posting on a new social contract, testing how an open protocol can enforce community rules.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 02:13 UTC#98c65b3 min read

Balaji Srinivasan’s Network School has acquired Farcaster from Neynar and plans to make acceptance of a new “social smart contract” the condition for posting and interacting, a sharp shift from the protocol’s earlier emphasis on letting users move among apps. Neynar co-founder Rish said the transfer includes the Farcaster app, protocol repositories and related products; in his Farcaster announcement, he said the company had received several proposals and that Farcaster needed a different operating model. The price was not disclosed.

The handoff comes less than nine months after Neynar took over from Farcaster’s original developer, Merkle Manufactory, in January. According to The Block’s report, most Neynar employees will join Network School to continue building the service, while Rish and fellow co-founder Manan are leaving. That preserves much of the team while moving control of the app and protocol repositories to a new operator.

What does the social contract require?

Users are asked to accept or decline the new rules. The Block reports that those who opt in receive an access NFT recording their choice onchain; users who decline retain read-only access through the main app, while their keys, funds, followers and post history are to move to another client. The rules list bannable conduct including hate speech, death threats, scams, stalking and presenting AI-generated content as one’s own.

The change makes access to Farcaster’s main app conditional on a community agreement, while giving users who refuse a route to keep their account data and continue reading. It also changes the practical meaning of portability: taking an identity and social graph elsewhere may preserve a user’s ability to leave, but it does not preserve full participation in the app they are leaving.

How does this differ from Farcaster’s earlier model?

Farcaster’s protocol documentation describes an architecture in which user identities are recorded through blockchain contracts, while posts and other social actions are signed messages stored and synchronized through hubs. It says users can choose among applications that connect to the network. The new access NFT adds an onchain record of consent, but the rules govern participation in the app; the announced mechanism does not put every post or moderation decision onchain.

That distinction matters against both the old Farcaster model and other approaches to decentralized social networking. Farcaster’s docs describe ActivityPub as a federation-based alternative and position Farcaster as a hybrid of blockchain identity and offchain social data. Network School’s plan adds a stronger operator-led rules layer to that hybrid: it may give the app clearer grounds to moderate conduct, while asking users to accept a central set of participation terms. A portable protocol can still support other clients, but the main app’s rules can make that exit costly in attention and community access.

What should users watch next?

Network School says Farcaster is becoming the “Network State Protocol,” with a goal of building opt-in digital societies that can extend into physical communities. The Block reports that the new app also plans to introduce human verification gradually to reduce bot activity. Those are intentions, not demonstrated outcomes. The next tests are how the contract is enforced, how users can transfer their accounts and data to another client, and whether human checks and moderation make the network more usable without weakening the choice of apps that its protocol was designed to support.
