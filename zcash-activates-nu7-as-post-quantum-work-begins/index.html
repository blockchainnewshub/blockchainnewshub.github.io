Zcash Activates NU7 as Post-Quantum Work Begins

Zcash’s NU7 upgrade cut target blocks to 25 seconds and redirected most transaction fees to a reserve, while a separate team began designing post-quantum cryptography.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 9, 2026 · 02:50 UTC#a257c43 min read

Zcash activated its NU7 upgrade at block 3,543,405 on Nov. 6, cutting its target time between blocks from 75 seconds to 25 and changing how transaction fees are handled. The faster schedule should reduce the wait for an initial confirmation, while limits on shielded actions are intended to contain added validation and synchronization work. CoinDesk Research’s report on NU7’s activation says the chain continued without a split; its early sample averaged about 39 seconds between blocks as difficulty adjusted. Separately, Shielded Labs has launched a research effort aimed at post-quantum cryptography, but that work is not part of NU7.

What does NU7 change for Zcash users and miners?

The upgrade makes blocks arrive at a target interval three times as often, which can bring a first confirmation sooner for users and merchants who accept one. It does not guarantee a 25-second payment: wallets and exchanges may require additional confirmations, and the actual interval can vary. The early 39-second average reported by CoinDesk suggests the network was still adjusting after activation.

More frequent blocks could also increase the work of validating and synchronizing shielded transactions. NU7 pairs the shorter interval with per-pool and global limits on shielded actions to bound those costs. The trade-off is a faster confirmation target with protocol limits on activity in each block; the upgrade does not remove the capacity constraints.

NU7 also moves 60% of transaction fees into a public reserve, leaving miners the other 40%. The reserve is intended to fund later mining rewards, with payouts scheduled to begin at block 8,939,664, expected around 2031. According to the official ZIP 259 deployment specification, the upgrade does not raise Zcash’s 21 million ZEC supply cap: block rewards are scaled down as blocks become more frequent, and the number of blocks between halvings rises accordingly.

Does the quantum effort make Zcash quantum-resistant now?

No. Shielded Labs says its new project, Epoch, is researching cryptography that could eventually replace quantum-vulnerable components. The three-person team is led by Chief Cryptographer Ulrich Haböck, with cryptography engineers Luke Edwards and Suyash Bagad. The group is targeting a production-ready implementation by the end of 2027, which could form the basis of a future network upgrade.

The organization says it will evaluate candidate designs against security and performance needs, aiming for at least 128 bits of security for confidentiality and soundness, along with formal verification. Those are goals, not a selected design or a deployed defense. Shielded Labs also says the Ironwood pool, introduced in the earlier NU6.3 upgrade, allows funds to be recovered through a future protocol if the current elliptic-curve cryptography must be disabled. It explicitly says this fallback does not make Zcash post-quantum today.

That distinction matters: NU7 changes block timing, transaction limits and fee handling; Epoch is a separate research program with a longer timeline and open design questions. Shielded Labs says Epoch’s work will complement Project Tachyon and other post-quantum efforts in the Zcash ecosystem.

What should readers watch after activation?

First, block intervals and confirmation waits will show whether the network approaches the new target and whether wallet providers pass the change through to users. Second, the reserve’s fee inflows and eventual reward schedule will show how the Network Sustainability Mechanism works in practice. Third, Epoch’s proposed cryptographic design, benchmarks and verification progress will indicate whether its 2027 target remains achievable. Until a design is implemented in a future upgrade, the quantum effort is preparation rather than present-day protection.
