Ethereum sets Dec. 15 Hoodi test for Glamsterdam

Ethereum developers rescheduled Glamsterdam on Hoodi for Dec. 15 and kept its gas changes intact, reopening testing after an October delay without setting a mainnet date.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 05:16 UTC#00e8913 min read

Ethereum developers have set Dec. 15 for Glamsterdam’s Hoodi testnet activation, replacing an October 26 date that was postponed while teams examined how the upgrade’s gas changes could affect existing smart contracts. The new schedule advances the test under the current specification, rather than waiting for changes to it; a mainnet date remains unset. The Block’s report on the developers’ decision puts activation at 19:12 UTC.

The shift resets the last public testnet step after the Ethereum Foundation said in October that the Hoodi fork would be delayed and no replacement date had been set. At that point, the Foundation cited further investigation into EIP-8037’s interactions with mainnet smart contracts. The original schedule also included a Hoodi gas-limit increase to 200 million, which was removed along with the fork configuration when the date was withdrawn, according to the Ethereum Foundation’s postponement notice.

Developers keep the gas proposal in scope

At the November 26 All Core Devs Consensus call, execution-layer teams reached rough consensus to proceed with the existing Glamsterdam specification, despite concerns about possible contract breakage and how the GAS opcode reports remaining transaction gas. That is the consequential choice behind the new date: teams could have changed the proposal or delayed again, but instead scheduled another test with EIP-8037 intact.

EIP-8037 raises the gas cost of creating new state, the data Ethereum nodes must store, and meters that cost separately from ordinary execution. Its aim is to limit state growth as block gas limits rise, helping contain the storage burden on node operators. The trade-off is that some existing contracts may rely on assumptions about gas accounting that the change disrupts. Decrypt reported that the proposal’s authors estimate creating a new account would cost about seven times more gas and deploying a 24-kilobyte contract about eight times more; they say the change targets state growth of roughly 120 GiB a year at a 150-million gas limit.

Glamsterdam also includes changes beyond gas pricing. Its headline features are enshrined proposer-builder separation, which brings the division between block proposers and builders into Ethereum’s protocol, and block-level access lists, which describe the state a block touches so clients can process transactions in parallel. Together, those changes are intended to support layer-one scaling, but the Hoodi test is where teams can assess whether the full upgrade works in a live public test environment. Glamsterdam has already activated on Sepolia, another Ethereum testnet; Hoodi is the next scheduled test, not a mainnet launch.

Hoodi activation is the next checkpoint

The November call also selected a less disruptive method for validating builder payloads: an approach using RLP for block-hash checks, instead of an alternative that would have required a consensus change. That choice favors a quicker path with less disruption to consensus, while leaving a more extensive change aside for now.

Client teams are expected to prepare releases for the Hoodi fork, and developers have not announced a mainnet activation date. The signals to watch are whether execution- and consensus-layer clients publish compatible releases, whether the Dec. 15 fork completes as scheduled, and whether testing surfaces contract or gas-accounting problems that reopen the EIP-8037 decision. Until those checks are clear, the date marks a testnet milestone rather than a committed Ethereum mainnet timetable.
