How to Swap Bridged ETH for SUI on a Sui DEX: Get SUI

How to swap bridged ETH for SUI on a Sui DEX: connect your wallet, review the route and minimum output, then sign and verify the SUI receipt.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 20, 2026 · 07:21 UTC#886c494 min read

A Sui DEX can swap bridged ETH for native SUI after the ETH reaches your Sui wallet. The bridge delivers an asset to Sui; the DEX trade is the separate step that exchanges that asset for spendable SUI. You’ll need a supported swap route and enough SUI for gas, unless the transaction has explicit sponsorship.

1. Start with the ETH received on Sui

The first step is to identify the bridged ETH in the Sui wallet that received it. Suibridge transfers supported assets from Ethereum to Sui through a lock-and-mint process, so the asset on Sui is represented as a Sui coin type. A DEX needs to trade the coin type you actually hold.

Open a Sui DEX that supports the asset and connect the receiving wallet on Sui mainnet. Cetus supports wallet connection and lets traders set slippage for a swap; its swap instructions describe that workflow. Confirm that the DEX lists the bridged ETH asset you received, rather than choosing a similarly named token by appearance alone.

Coin type matters because bridged assets can have distinct on-chain identities. For a closer explanation of what Suibridge-delivered tokens represent on Sui, see Suibridge token backing and coin types. That context helps you compare the wallet’s received asset with the DEX’s input selection before trading.

2. Set bridged ETH as input and SUI as output

Select the received bridged ETH as the input and native SUI as the output. Enter the amount you want to trade, checking that the input is drawn from the connected wallet and that the output is SUI. This is the actual exchange: the bridge transfer alone does not convert the delivered asset into native SUI.

A route may pass through an intermediate token when the DEX’s available pools use that path. Cetus’s aggregator supports routing through intermediate pools in multi-hop swaps. The practical gain is that the DEX can use those pools within one transaction, so you do not need to make a separate purchase of the intermediate token first. The route is still worth reviewing: a path with more than one pool can differ from a direct route in price and execution terms.

3. Review the quote before signing

The quote shows the proposed route and the expected SUI output for your chosen amount. Review the price impact, minimum SUI output after slippage, and gas before signing. Slippage is the allowance for the output to move between quoting and execution; a wider setting can allow a less favorable result, while a tighter one can make the transaction less likely to complete if the price changes.

Compare the minimum output with the amount of SUI you are willing to receive. Also account for gas: the wallet needs enough SUI to cover it, or the transaction must have explicit sponsorship. The expected output and the minimum output answer different questions. The former is the quote; the latter is the stated floor under the chosen slippage setting.

Pause if the DEX shows no compatible route, the selected input does not match the received coin type, or the minimum output is below what you accept. Recheck the token selection and amount, then request a fresh quote. A swap can only deliver the result described by an available route and the terms you approve.

4. Sign and verify SUI arrived

Sign only when the route, minimum SUI output, and gas terms are acceptable. After execution, verify that the transaction succeeded and that SUI appears in the receiving wallet, with the gas cost accounted for. A successful transaction and credited SUI are the completion check; a submitted signature by itself is not confirmation that the intended output arrived.

Keep the final check tied to the wallet and transaction you used: confirm the credited SUI balance and consider the gas spent when judging the net amount received. Before signing, match the input coin type to the ETH you received; that simple check helps avoid quoting a different token with a similar name.
