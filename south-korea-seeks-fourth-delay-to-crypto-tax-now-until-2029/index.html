South Korea Seeks Fourth Delay to Crypto Tax, Now Until 2029

Seoul will seek to move crypto taxation from 2027 to 2029, citing missing US transaction data and rule changes, while proposing three-year loss offsets.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 5, 2026 · 19:13 UTC#4614163 min read

South Korea’s government will ask lawmakers to shift cryptocurrency income taxation from January 2027 to 2029, extending a system already delayed three times rather than starting it on the current schedule. The Yonhap report on Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il’s announcement says the government wants the additional time to prepare for US tax information exchanges and changes to digital asset rules. The delay remains a proposal: the National Assembly must amend the Income Tax Act for it to take effect.

Under the existing plan, individuals would pay 22%, including local income tax, on annual cryptocurrency gains above 2.5 million won (about US$1,869), according to Yonhap. The scheme was legislated in 2020 for a January 2022 start, then postponed; 2027 is its scheduled start before this latest proposal. Moving it again gives the government more time to build reporting systems, but leaves investors and exchanges without a settled start date until lawmakers act.

Why does Seoul want to wait until 2029?

The government says 2029 is when virtual asset information exchanges with the United States are due to begin. Without those records, the ministry argues, taxing domestic trades first could put South Korea’s market at a disadvantage and make it harder to assess overseas transactions fairly. In its November 5 briefing on virtual asset taxation, the Ministry of Finance and Economy also pointed to expected changes to the industry’s regulatory framework under a proposed digital asset law.

The choice is between proceeding in 2027 with the current rules and data gaps, or using two more years to prepare for broader reporting and a changing regulatory framework. The first option would start taxing gains sooner; the second could improve the government’s ability to account for overseas trading, but delays collection and prolongs uncertainty over implementation. The ministry says it will monitor international information-sharing arrangements and work with the Financial Services Commission to advance the digital asset law.

What changes could investors see alongside the delay?

The government also plans to seek a three-year loss carryforward, allowing investors to offset future gains with eligible losses. At present, the planned system allows losses to offset gains only within the same year. Because crypto prices can fluctuate sharply between tax years, the proposed change could make taxable income reflect gains and losses across a longer period. Yonhap reports that the government pointed to similar practices in the United States and Britain; the proposed carryforward would still require legislation.

A public-private working group is expected to review rules for different transaction types and ways to make filing easier. The ministry plans to discuss legislative amendments with lawmakers in the 2027 regular session and publish detailed tax guidance in the first half of 2028. The schedule gives authorities time to clarify how the tax will apply, while asking investors to wait longer for a final framework.

What should investors watch next?

The immediate test is whether the National Assembly amends the Income Tax Act before the currently scheduled start. After that, the key signals are whether US information exchange begins on time in 2029, whether lawmakers approve loss carryforwards, and whether the planned guidance resolves how overseas trades and different transaction types will be reported. Until those steps are settled, 2029 is the government’s target, not a completed change in the law.
