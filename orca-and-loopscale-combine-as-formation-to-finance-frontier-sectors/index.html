Orca and Loopscale combine as Formation to finance frontier sectors

Orca and Loopscale have merged as Formation, pairing Solana trading with credit and vaults to finance frontier businesses while keeping both token networks in place.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 14, 2026 · 09:27 UTC#ba46533 min read

Solana decentralized exchange Orca and lending platform Loopscale merged on Oct. 7 under the name Formation, combining trading, credit and investment vaults to help finance businesses in AI, energy, robotics and defense. The merger brings together functions the two protocols had offered separately; Orca’s announcement distributed on PR Newswire says the existing Orca and Loopscale protocols will remain foundations of the ORCA and xORCA token network.

Formation’s pitch is that an emerging asset needs more than a trading venue: it also needs credit and distribution. Orca contributes trading and liquidity infrastructure, while Loopscale brings lending and vault strategies. That could give an issuer a route from launch toward financing and a market within one combined operation. It also shifts the teams’ focus beyond crypto trading and lending toward capital-intensive industries, though the announcement describes plans and intended capabilities rather than completed financing deals in those sectors.

What does Formation combine that Orca and Loopscale offered separately?

Orca has processed more than $550 billion in trading volume since 2021, according to the companies’ announcement. Loopscale reports more than $150 million in deposits and over $2 billion in loans facilitated. Those figures describe activity on the separate platforms, not results from Formation’s new combined services.

Loopscale co-founder Luke Truitt will lead Formation as CEO. Orca’s Christopher Montagano will serve as chief strategy and legal officer, and Loopscale co-founder Mary Gooneratne as chief operating officer. Over the next 12 months, Formation says it plans to introduce tools for asset issuers and new capital-allocation strategies alongside its existing trading, lending and vault offerings. It says it is already working with Figure, Shinhan Asset Management, Superstate, R3 and Securitize to bring assets to market and expand distribution.

How does its financing pitch compare with conventional markets?

Formation argues that fixed underwriting, legal, servicing and distribution costs in traditional capital markets can make smaller or less standardized deals difficult to finance. Its proposed alternative is to use onchain trading, credit and vault infrastructure together, reducing the cost and complexity of structuring and distributing assets. The potential gain is a more direct path between issuers and capital allocators; the trade-off is that the combined infrastructure still has to demonstrate that it can attract issuers, lenders and buyers for assets beyond crypto.

The company says it aims eventually to offer access to regulated U.S. capital markets. That is a stated direction, not a confirmed launch or authorization. The announcement does not specify which assets will be available through any future regulated offering, when one might open, or what legal and operational steps Formation will need to complete.

What should ORCA and xORCA holders watch next?

The report by The Block says the financial terms of the merger were not disclosed. The announcement confirms that both protocols remain part of the ORCA and xORCA token network, but does not detail how the merger affects tokenholder economics or governance. Holders therefore have confirmation of continuity at the protocol level, not a full account of the deal’s financial implications.

The next signals are whether Formation delivers its planned issuer tools and investment strategies within the stated 12-month window, which assets and partners it brings to market, and whether its combined credit and trading setup can reach regulated U.S. markets. The announcement lays out the intended route; execution will show whether the merger can turn two established crypto platforms into financing infrastructure for businesses outside the sector.
