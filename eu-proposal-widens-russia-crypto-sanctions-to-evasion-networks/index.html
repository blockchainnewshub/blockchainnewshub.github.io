EU proposal widens Russia crypto sanctions to evasion networks

The Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal would extend bank bans and target crypto networks, widening pressure beyond Russia to third countries aiding evasion.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 07:45 UTC#00ddb03 min read

The European Commission’s proposed 22nd Russia sanctions package would extend transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and target crypto and payment networks, widening the effort beyond Russia to third countries that help it evade sanctions. The Commission’s statement on the proposal sets out a broader financial focus than a bank-by-bank measure alone: it couples the restrictions with a new Crypto Sanctions Task Force.

The shift is proposed, not yet in force. The Associated Press reports that all 27 EU member countries must endorse the package before it can take effect; that process can take weeks, and the planned measures could change. The distinction matters for crypto businesses and counterparties: the Commission has announced its intended reach, but not a final list of affected platforms or detailed rules for each transaction.

From named platforms to networks beyond Russia

The latest adopted package, the EU’s 21st round in July, already tightened financial and crypto restrictions. The Council said it imposed transaction bans on 14 crypto-related service platforms and introduced the possibility of a full third-country ban on crypto-asset services used by Russia to evade EU sanctions. The new proposal puts Russia and third-country evasion networks at the centre of its stated crypto focus, while adding a task force to support enforcement. That points to a wider enforcement effort, though the Commission’s statement does not specify which providers or transactions would be caught.

The figures show the scale and the limits of what is known so far:

  • About 60 Russian banks: The Commission proposes extending the transaction ban to them and removing all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments.
  • Crypto and payment networks: The proposal targets networks in Russia and in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. The Commission did not name the networks in its statement.
  • A Crypto Sanctions Task Force: The Commission says it has launched the group to support this work, signalling an enforcement component alongside the proposed restrictions.

For crypto firms, the central trade-off is between a wider perimeter for sanctions enforcement and less certainty about which cross-border services will be affected. Reaching networks outside Russia could address routes that country-specific restrictions leave open. But until the proposal becomes a final measure and its scope is spelled out, operators cannot tell from the announcement alone which links or services may be prohibited. The Commission says it wants to align this work with international partners, but gave no further detail in its statement.

Adoption is the next test

The package also proposes listing more than 70 additional vessels in Russia’s shadow fleet and banning Russian teams and athletes from competing under their country’s flag at EU sports events. These measures show the proposal’s reach across finance, energy and public life; for crypto, the key test is whether the member states keep the third-country focus and how they define the networks covered.

The Associated Press report on the proposed package says the approval process can take weeks and the measures may be modified. The signals to watch are whether the 27 governments agree, whether the final text names crypto providers or sets a broader transaction rule, and how the task force coordinates with international partners. Until those details emerge, the announcement marks an expansion of the EU’s intended approach, not a new restriction already binding crypto markets.
