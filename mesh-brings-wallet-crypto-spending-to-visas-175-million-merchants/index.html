Mesh brings wallet crypto spending to Visa’s 175 million merchants

Mesh will connect crypto wallets to Visa merchants without separate cards, but platform participation, supported devices and launch timing remain unannounced.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 20, 2026 · 04:25 UTC#7604e83 min read

Mesh and Visa announced a partnership on Oct. 19 to let people pay from crypto and stablecoin balances in participating wallet apps, bringing those balances to Visa’s network of more than 175 million merchant locations across over 200 countries and territories. The Mesh announcement says users would not need to sell, transfer or preload funds before paying. That cuts steps compared with cashing out to a bank account or signing up for a card tied to one platform, the routes Mesh says consumers use today. But the partnership is an integration plan: the companies have not named launch dates, wallet partners or supported devices.

How would a wallet payment reach Visa’s network?

Mesh says it will orchestrate funding and conversion across supported crypto, stablecoin and fiat balances. A Visa payment credential would be provisioned behind the scenes in a participating app; users could tap at a contactless terminal or pay online, while merchants would receive a standard Visa payment without changing checkout hardware or procedures. The The Block report on the partnership also notes that rollout details have yet to be announced.

That mechanism shifts the work from the consumer to the payment infrastructure. A wallet platform could connect through Mesh instead of building and operating its own card program, while Mesh says it will manage onboarding and know-your-customer requirements. The announcement names Kalshi, Tools for Humanity, Félix Pago, Majority and KAST as existing Mesh partners, but does not say which will offer the Visa payment feature first. A broad acceptance network is therefore not the same as immediate access for every wallet user.

What changes compared with existing crypto cards?

Many crypto payment products have linked a balance to a card, often requiring sign-up with a particular platform or a transfer before spending. Mesh’s proposed flow keeps the balance in the wallet app until a payment is made and places a Visa credential inside that experience. The gain is fewer steps and a familiar merchant checkout; the trade-off is that the transaction still relies on Mesh to fund and convert the payment, and on Visa’s network to carry it. This is not a promise that merchants will receive crypto directly.

Visa has pursued adjacent routes: The Block reported that Visa and Bridge partnered last year on cards funded by stablecoin balances, while Visa and WeFi planned a way to spend digital assets through Visa. Mesh’s pitch differs in putting the credential within participating wallet apps and in letting platforms connect through Mesh rather than run a card program themselves. The announcement does not detail conversion rates, fees, eligible assets or how users choose which balance to spend, so the practical differences will depend on product terms that have not been published.

What needs to be confirmed before users can rely on it?

The next test is rollout: which wallets and platforms integrate first, where the service launches, which devices it supports and when payments become available. Those details will show whether the partnership reaches users across multiple wallets as Mesh envisages, or remains limited to a smaller set of platforms. Fees, supported balances and the conversion process will also determine whether avoiding a separate card makes everyday spending simpler in practice. For now, the announcement establishes a payment route and a merchant-network reach; it does not establish a live consumer service.
