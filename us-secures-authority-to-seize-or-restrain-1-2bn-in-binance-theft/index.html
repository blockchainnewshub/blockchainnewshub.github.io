US secures authority to seize or restrain $1.2bn in Binance theft

US authorities won court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of stolen Binance crypto, while efforts to freeze and recover the rest continue.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 15, 2026 · 03:35 UTC#44088c3 min read

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a legal step beyond the broader freezes already imposed on funds from the theft, but not confirmation that all of that crypto has been transferred into government custody. The Justice Department said on Nov. 12 that more than $2.7 billion had been frozen through joint government and private-sector action; its announcement says the court-authorized amount is part of that total.

The distinction matters. A freeze can block movement while investigators and service providers trace assets; seizure or restraint authority gives the government a legal route to hold property, but does not itself settle who ultimately owns it. The Associated Press reported the FBI’s announcement as a seizure of more than $1.2 billion. The Justice Department’s more specific account describes court authorization to “seize or restrain” that amount, while saying the wider recovery effort is ongoing.

From a $6 billion theft to coordinated freezes

The FBI attributes the theft, which the Justice Department says took place on or about Oct. 29, to members of the North Korean military known as TraderTraitor. The department puts the amount stolen at more than $6 billion. The AP has described it as the biggest crypto heist in history. Those figures put the court-authorized amount in perspective: it is a substantial portion of the reported loss, but it does not account for all the stolen assets.

Authorities say they have worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to intercept the funds. That approach depends on cooperation across the crypto ecosystem to identify and freeze assets as they move. According to the Justice Department, more than $2.7 billion has been frozen in total, with court authorization covering more than $1.2 billion of that sum. The figures describe different stages of the response: a freeze is a block on movement, while seizure and restraint are legal measures pursued through the courts.

The remaining assets have not simply disappeared from view. The department says TraderTraitor actors continue laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued a cyber alert attributing the theft and urging VASPs to block transactions involving, or derived from, addresses the actors are using to launder the Binance funds.

Binance’s response also sets this recovery effort apart from a loss left entirely to customers. The AP reported that the exchange said it had reserved more than $6.34 billion in company funds to cover the stolen assets and had begun recovering assets for users. That commitment addresses customer losses, while the government’s action targets the stolen crypto itself. Recovery and compensation are related, but the sources do not say that the court-authorized funds have already been returned to Binance or its users.

Forfeiture and tracing remain ahead

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets continue. In a typical forfeiture process, the government files a claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds, the AP reported. The department has not said in its announcement that this process is complete for the more than $1.2 billion covered by the authorization.

The next signals are whether authorities convert the court authorization into completed seizures, how much of the wider frozen total can be forfeited and returned, and whether investigators or service providers can intercept funds still moving through bridges, mixers and exchanges. For now, the scale of the recovery is clear; the final amount recovered and its destination are not.
