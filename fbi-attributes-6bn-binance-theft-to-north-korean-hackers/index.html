FBI attributes $6bn Binance theft to North Korean hackers

The FBI linked Binance’s October 29 theft of more than $6 billion to North Korea’s TraderTraitor, as funds move into Ethereum and laundering routes.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 1, 2026 · 08:55 UTC#f656f04 min read

The FBI has attributed the theft of more than $6 billion from Binance to North Korea, identifying the operation as TraderTraitor and warning that the stolen assets are moving toward laundering routes. In its October 31 cyber alert, the bureau said the theft occurred on or about October 29. The scale surpasses the roughly $1.5 billion Bybit theft that the FBI attributed to the same actors in 2025, making this the largest crypto heist reported to date.

The attribution changes the story from an exchange investigating a security incident to a law enforcement effort to disrupt the movement of funds. The FBI, working with the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, said it had observed the actors converting assets primarily into Ethereum. Binance has said it will cover customers’ losses, but tracing and blocking the funds will determine whether any can be recovered before they move through additional services.

How does the Binance theft compare with the Bybit hack?

At more than $6 billion, Binance’s loss is over four times the $1.5 billion Bybit theft attributed to TraderTraitor in February 2025. The FBI names the same operation in both cases, but the figures mark a substantial increase in the amount stolen in a single incident. The Binance attack took place on or about October 29; the bureau’s notice does not give a precise duration for the theft.

Binance’s initial account points to a possible route into its systems. The company told the Associated Press that its investigation suggested a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop may have allowed an attacker to access a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. Binance said it had found no evidence that wallet private keys were leaked. It has not yet published final technical findings, so the suspected laptop route remains an early assessment rather than a confirmed explanation of the breach.

The distinction matters: a compromised operational device and unauthorized withdrawal process would imply a different failure path from stolen wallet keys. Binance said deposits and withdrawals were temporarily suspended during security checks and that blockchain partners had helped freeze affected assets. The company also said it would fully reimburse customers, and AP reported that Binance had enough of the same digital currency set aside to cover 90% of the missing assets.

Can investigators stop the funds from being laundered?

The FBI’s immediate appeal is to exchanges, blockchain infrastructure operators, analytics firms and DeFi services: block transactions linked to the wallet addresses listed in its alert. The bureau said it expects the actors to use DeFi platforms and instant swap services for further laundering. That creates a race between public blockchain visibility, which lets investigators track transfers, and the speed with which funds can be split and moved across services.

The trade-off is clear. Public ledgers give investigators and service providers transaction trails they can use to flag and freeze assets, but they do not guarantee that funds can be recovered once they pass through multiple intermediaries. The FBI’s warning gives private firms specific addresses to monitor; the attackers can still try to route assets through services that do not respond to those requests.

What should Binance customers and the market watch next?

The next signals are whether exchanges and DeFi services block the listed addresses, how much of the stolen value can be frozen, and whether Binance’s final technical account confirms the suspected access route. In its report on the FBI attribution, AP said Binance promised full customer reimbursement. That commitment limits the direct loss customers may face, but it does not settle how the breach happened or prevent a repeat. The pace of transfers into Ethereum and any subsequent movement through instant swaps will show whether investigators can interrupt the laundering before the funds become harder to trace.
