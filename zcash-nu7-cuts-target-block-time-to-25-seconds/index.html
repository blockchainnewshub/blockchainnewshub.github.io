Zcash NU7 cuts target block time to 25 seconds

Zcash activated NU7 at block 3,543,405, cutting target block times to 25 seconds while changing fee flows and ending spends from its oldest pool.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 7, 2026 · 00:18 UTC#4ddc6e3 min read

Zcash activated its NU7 network upgrade at block 3,543,405 on Nov. 6 UTC, cutting target block spacing from 75 seconds to 25 and potentially making shielded payments confirm sooner. The Zcash Foundation’s Zebra 7.0.0 release notes describe the change as a threefold reduction in the average wait for a first confirmation; the upgrade also changes how fees are reserved and limits shielded activity per block.

The shorter interval is a change in the network’s target, not a promise that every payment is spendable in 25 seconds. In the first hours after activation, blocks averaged about 39 seconds apart as mining difficulty adjusted, according to CoinDesk’s report on NU7’s launch. Merchants, exchanges and other services set their own confirmation requirements, so their actual wait depends on those policies as well as block production.

How much faster are private payments expected to be?

For a first confirmation, the target wait falls from 75 seconds to 25. A service that waits for three confirmations could see its expected wait fall from nearly four minutes to about 75 seconds, though the service decides when to credit or release funds. The change matters for shielded transfers, which conceal sender, recipient and amount, but it does not remove the need to wait for confirmations.

More frequent blocks also leave less time for miners to receive and check each block before another is found. NU7 pairs the faster schedule with transaction checks that can reuse some work already done when a transaction first arrives. In a test of 25-second blocks across 80 computers in 11 regions, 3.43% of blocks were discarded, compared with 1.57% at the previous pace, CoinDesk reported. That test illustrates a trade-off under demanding conditions; it is not a measure of the live network’s long-run performance.

What changes for capacity, fees and old ZEC?

NU7 places per-block limits on activity in Orchard, Ironwood and Sapling, Zcash’s shielded pools. The Foundation says the limits raise estimated Orchard throughput from 2.9 to 6.6 transactions per second while cutting by about 37% the worst-case sync bandwidth a spammer could impose on light wallets. That could support more simple private payments and limit the work wallets must do, while restricting how much shielded activity can be packed into an individual block.

The Network Sustainability Mechanism changes the fee split: miners keep 40% of each block’s transaction fees, while 60% goes into a reserve for later block rewards. The reserve is scheduled to begin re-entering rewards at block 8,939,664, expected in 2031. Block rewards per block are reduced to offset the faster schedule, preserving the existing halving timetable and 21 million ZEC supply cap. The mechanism sets aside fee revenue for future security, but its value to miners depends on future fees and the scheduled reserve releases.

NU7 also makes version 4 transactions invalid. Because Zcash’s original Sprout pool can only use that format, about 12,224 ZEC left there at activation became unspendable. Newer version 5 and 6 transactions remain valid, according to the Foundation’s release notes. The upgrade therefore speeds the network while closing off a legacy route to funds that were not moved in time.

What should users and operators watch next?

The key signals are whether block intervals settle near the new 25-second target, whether shielded throughput rises without increasing wallet sync burdens, and how services adjust their confirmation policies. Node operators also had to upgrade before activation to remain on the consensus chain. For users, service support and transaction type determine the practical effect: NU7 changes the underlying network, but does not make every wallet, exchange or merchant act faster automatically.
