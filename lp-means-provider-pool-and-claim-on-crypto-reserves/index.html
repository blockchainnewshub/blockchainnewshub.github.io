LP Means Provider, Pool and Claim on Crypto Reserves

What does LP mean in crypto? It can mean a liquidity provider, a liquidity pool or the LP tokens that represent a provider’s share of its reserves.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 23, 2026 · 20:43 UTC#c3d0ad5 min read

In crypto, LP means liquidity provider, liquidity pool or LP token, depending on the page; the terms connect because providers deposit assets into pools and receive tokens representing their share. The rugcheck topic explains how a token risk check reports signs that may point to a scam or rug pull. Decentralized exchanges use pooled reserves for swaps instead of matching buyers and sellers through an order book, so “LP” can refer to the person supplying those reserves, the pool itself or the claim on it.

LP tokens represent a share of a pool’s reserves

A liquidity provider deposits two assets into a pool, and the exchange uses those pooled assets to trade. The provider receives LP tokens in return. Those tokens track the provider’s share of the pool and can be redeemed later for that share of both assets.

Take an illustrative Raydium pool for SOL and a memecoin. A provider deposits equal values of SOL and the memecoin, then receives LP tokens. The tokens represent a proportion of the pool, rather than a fixed number of each asset. The exact share depends on how much of the pool the deposit represents.

Each swap changes the quantities of the two assets in the pool. On Raydium’s standard automated market maker pools, the constant-product formula x·y=k describes how the reserves relate: as traders take one asset out, they add the other, shifting the reserve ratio and moving the price. Each swap also adds a fee to the reserves.

That shift matters when the provider withdraws. Redeeming LP tokens returns the provider’s share of both reserves at the current ratio, not necessarily the original quantities deposited. Swap fees remain in the pool, so each LP token can redeem for more of the reserves over time. But because trading changes the ratio, the mix returned may differ from the initial deposit.

The three common meanings are related but do different jobs:

  • Liquidity provider: the person or account depositing assets to make swaps possible.
  • Liquidity pool: the reserves traders swap against on a decentralized exchange.
  • LP token: the token issued for a deposit, representing a share that can be redeemed.

When a wallet or token page says “LP,” use the surrounding words to identify which meaning fits. “LP tokens” points to the claim; “LP locked” or “LP burned” describes what happened to those tokens. A page that says a project added LP may mean it supplied assets to a pool.

“LP locked” and “LP burned” describe the claim tokens

“LP locked” means some LP tokens have been sent to a lock, usually for a stated period. Because those tokens control a share of the pool, the lock can restrict when that share is withdrawn. “LP burned” means tokens have been sent to an address from which they cannot be spent, removing that claim from circulation.

On a token risk page, the locked percentage generally counts LP tokens, not the pool’s reserves directly. That distinction is useful: the tokens represent claims on reserves, but a percentage of tokens is not the same figure as a percentage of the reserves by asset value. Read the page’s labels and details to see which tokens it counts and whether the lock has an expiry.

A rugcheck may report what share of a token’s LP tokens is locked or burned because those claims determine who can withdraw the pool’s assets. That information helps explain one part of the token’s setup. It describes the reported LP-token status; the reader still needs to understand what the page means by “locked” and check the relevant details.

What is impermanent loss?

Impermanent loss is the difference between the value of assets held in a liquidity pool and the value of simply holding the original deposited assets, when the assets’ prices move relative to each other. Pool trades rebalance the reserves, so a provider’s share can end up with a different mix than the deposit. Fees added to the pool may offset some of that difference, but how much depends on trading and price changes.

For the SOL and memecoin example, imagine SOL’s price changes substantially against the memecoin while swaps keep adjusting the pool’s ratio. On withdrawal, the provider receives their current share of both reserves, plus the effect of fees in the pool. The value can differ from holding the original SOL and memecoin outside the pool. This comparison is what “impermanent loss” names; it is not a separate withdrawal fee.

What does “LP burned” mean on a token page?

It means LP tokens representing a claim on the pool have been sent somewhere they cannot be spent. Since those tokens determine who can withdraw that share of the reserves, burning them removes that claim from circulation. Check the page’s details to understand which LP tokens it counts.

What is impermanent loss?

It is the difference between the pool share’s value and the value of holding the original deposit as prices move. The pool’s changing reserve ratio affects what comes back on withdrawal; swap fees stay in reserves and affect the value of the share.

When “LP” appears on a token or risk page, first identify whether it means the provider, the pool or the claim tokens. Then read any locked or burned percentage as a statement about LP tokens and check the reported lock details. That gives you a clearer picture of who can withdraw the pool’s reserves and what the page’s figure actually measures.
