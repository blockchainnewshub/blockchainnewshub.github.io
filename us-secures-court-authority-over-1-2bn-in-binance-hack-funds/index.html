US secures court authority over $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain $1.2bn of Binance’s stolen crypto, part of $2.7bn frozen as tracing and recovery continue.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 20:51 UTC#c1fd4b3 min read

US authorities have obtained court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, shifting a portion of the funds from a broad freeze effort into a court-authorized process. The Justice Department says government and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total; the approved amount is part of that sum, not an additional recovery.

The Justice Department’s announcement on the seizure and restraint says the assets were taken in a theft from Binance on or about Oct. 29. It attributes the theft to members of the North Korean military whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor. The department said more than $6 billion in virtual assets was stolen. The Associated Press, reporting the FBI’s announcement, put the theft at about $6.3 billion.

From a freeze to court-authorized action

The distinction between frozen and seized funds matters. The combined freeze figure reflects work by US agencies and private-sector partners to stop assets moving; the Justice Department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion within that pool. The announcement does not say the full amount has been returned to Binance or its users, nor does it detail the assets’ specific tokens or addresses.

The response relies in part on cooperation with virtual asset service providers. The Justice Department says investigators and partners have worked with those firms to interdict the funds. It also says the stolen assets are still being laundered through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert attributing the theft to TraderTraitor and urging providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder Binance’s stolen assets.

That approach offers a faster way to impede transfers than waiting for funds to reach a conventional financial institution, but it depends on identifying addresses and on services being able to act. A freeze can interrupt movement without settling who ultimately owns the assets. As AP notes, the US government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines whether there is a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The announcement describes court authorization to seize or restrain assets; it does not announce a final forfeiture ruling or a distribution to victims.

Recovery remains short of the theft total

More than $2.7 billion frozen is a substantial share of a theft estimated at $6.3 billion by AP, but the figures describe different stages and should not be read as a final recovery tally. The $1.2 billion under court authorization represents less than half of the frozen amount, while the rest remains within a broader freeze effort. The announcement does not specify how much of the total theft remains accessible to the attackers.

The scale also exceeds the $1.5 billion stolen from the exchange Bybit in February 2025, which AP reported was attributed by the FBI to North Korea and TraderTraitor. Binance’s case is larger by reported dollar value, while the current action shows how cooperation between investigators and crypto services can limit movement before a forfeiture case is resolved.

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance funds are continuing, and the FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office is coordinating recovery with the Secret Service and IRS-CI. The next signals are whether investigators identify further assets, whether additional court actions follow, and whether a forfeiture process establishes who can claim the funds. Until then, the court authorization marks progress in containment, not proof that the stolen crypto has been returned.
