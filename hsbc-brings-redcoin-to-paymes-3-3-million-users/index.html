HSBC brings RedCoin to PayMe’s 3.3 million users

HSBC has launched RedCoin through PayMe for transfers and merchant payments, bringing a bank-issued HKD token to retail users ahead of planned investment uses.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 24, 2026 · 13:04 UTC#a6f4b83 min read

HSBC launched its first Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD), on PayMe on 23 October, extending its digital-asset offering from products such as retail gold tokens and institutional digital bonds to everyday payments. The bank says more than 3.3 million PayMe users will be able to activate wallets in the coming days for transfers and payments at more than 100,000 merchants. In its launch announcement, HSBC describes the coin as licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); the rollout makes a regulated bank-issued HKD token available through a consumer payments app.

What can PayMe users do with RedCoin at launch?

Users can send RedCoin to friends and family and pay merchants through PayMe. HSBC says nearly 100 staff tested the customer journey before launch, including minting, wallet activation and live transfers. The company has not presented the rollout as a change to every PayMe transaction: the stablecoin wallet is an added option, while the existing payment app remains the point of access.

The route is designed for reach through an existing customer base. HSBC says its wallet will be available to more than 3.3 million PayMe users, with no minimum balance or additional account-opening requirement reported by the South China Morning Post. That lowers the first step for customers already using the app. At launch, however, the stated uses are limited to PayMe transfers and merchant payments; the broader investment and wholesale applications are plans for later.

How does RedCoin compare with Hong Kong’s other licensed stablecoin?

RedCoin’s retail-first distribution differs from the first announced phase of HKDAP, the stablecoin issued by Anchorpoint Financial. The HKMA licensed both HSBC and Anchorpoint on 10 April, the first two issuers under the city’s regime. Anchorpoint began HKDAP beta access in August for institutional distributors and professional investors, with initial uses focused on cross-border payments and tokenised-asset settlement, according to The Block’s report on that rollout.

The approaches put different starting users first: PayMe consumers for RedCoin, institutions and professional investors for HKDAP’s initial phase. HSBC’s route puts stablecoin payments inside a familiar consumer service; HKDAP’s initial route uses authorised distributors to connect the token to commercial and financial applications. These are starting strategies rather than evidence of which model will attract more use. Both issuers operate under Hong Kong’s licensing framework, which the HKMA says requires controls for reserves, redemption, technology risk and anti-money-laundering.

What has HSBC committed to add, and what remains to be seen?

HSBC says it plans later this year to add RedCoin wallet functionality to the HSBC HK Mobile Banking App and let customers subscribe to tokenised investment products, including a planned share class of its Hong Kong dollar money market fund. It also says it intends to expand into wholesale corporate and institutional uses. Those extensions could widen RedCoin beyond routine payments, but they are future plans, not features confirmed as live at launch.

The immediate trade-off is clear in the product’s scope: PayMe offers a direct path to retail payments, while investment and institutional functions depend on later additions. HSBC has not detailed in its launch announcement a blockchain network or external-wallet access, leaving the extent of RedCoin’s interoperability unclear. The next signals are wallet activation across PayMe, actual use at the stated merchant network, the arrival of the HSBC HK App wallet and tokenised fund share class, and any announced wholesale integrations.
