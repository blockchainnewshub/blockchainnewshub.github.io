Zcash activates NU7, cutting target block time to 25 seconds

Zcash activated NU7 at block 3,543,405, tripling its target block pace while changing shielded capacity, fee handling and support for legacy Sprout funds.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 9, 2026 · 01:35 UTC#4db9683 min read

Zcash activated its NU7 upgrade at block 3,543,405 on November 5, cutting target block spacing from 75 seconds to 25 seconds and changing how the network handles shielded activity and transaction fees. CoinDesk’s report on the activation says blocks initially averaged about 39 seconds as mining difficulty adjusted, so the shorter target does not mean every payment is immediately confirmed in 25 seconds.

How much faster can private payments become?

The threefold reduction in target spacing lowers the expected wait for an initial confirmation, but services decide how many confirmations they require before crediting or releasing funds. CoinDesk estimated that a service accepting one confirmation could see the wait fall from around 75 seconds to 25, while one requiring three could move from nearly four minutes to about 75 seconds. Those are estimates based on target spacing; actual timing and a customer’s wait depend on block production and the service’s policy.

NU7 also changes the load shielded transactions place on wallets. Wallets scan blocks to find payments addressed to them, and a surge of private transactions can make that scanning heavier. According to the Zcash Foundation’s Zebra 7.0.0 release notes, NU7 sets per-block limits for Orchard, Ironwood and Sapling activity. The specification estimates that the limits reduce worst-case light-wallet download burden by about 37%, from 271 megabytes a day to 169, while increasing potential Orchard throughput from 2.9 to 6.6 transactions per second.

That is a capacity and wallet-sync trade-off: the network can accommodate more simple Orchard payments while bounding the work a flood of shielded activity could impose on wallets. These figures describe protocol limits and estimated capacity, not observed everyday payment volume.

What does NU7 change for miners and older funds?

Faster blocks could leave miners less time to receive and validate a new block before another competing block appears. In an 80-computer test across 11 regions, with blocks filled with transactions, Zakura’s engineers measured 3.43% of blocks discarded at 25-second spacing, versus 1.57% at the former 75-second pace. That test shows a cost of compressing intervals under demanding conditions; it does not establish the rate the live network will sustain.

NU7 adjusts block rewards to one-third their prior size and triples the number of blocks between halvings, keeping issuance per day and the four-year halving schedule unchanged. It also places 60% of each block’s transaction fees into a reserve and leaves miners the other 40%. The reserve is scheduled to begin returning gradually to future block rewards around 2031, preparing for lower block subsidies while preserving Zcash’s 21 million ZEC supply cap.

The transition also ends support for version 4 transactions. Since the old Sprout privacy pool can only spend through that format, approximately 12,224 ZEC remaining there became unspendable at activation. That is a concrete cost of retiring legacy transaction support; CoinDesk reported that no recovery plan had been approved as of November 6.

Which signals will show whether the change is working?

The next evidence is whether actual block intervals settle toward the new target, whether discarded-block rates remain manageable, and whether payment services adjust their confirmation policies. Shielded transaction throughput and wallet download demands will show whether the capacity limits deliver their intended balance in use. For the fee mechanism, the longer test is whether the reserve begins reissuing as scheduled around 2031 and contributes meaningfully as block rewards decline.
