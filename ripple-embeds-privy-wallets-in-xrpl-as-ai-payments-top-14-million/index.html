Ripple embeds Privy wallets in XRPL as AI payments top 14 million

Ripple and Privy are bringing embedded wallets to XRPL as its AI-payment dashboard tops 14 million transactions, widening access while leaving scale and value unresolved.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 08:35 UTC#0c5d234 min read

Ripple and Stripe-owned Privy are bringing embedded wallets to the XRP Ledger (XRPL), letting developers build apps where users can hold and send XRP, RLUSD and other ledger assets without setting up a separate crypto wallet. Privy announced the partnership on Oct. 13, saying its infrastructure will support wallet creation, transaction signing and x402 payments for software agents. That shifts the wallet setup into the app itself; it does not change the ledger or the assets being transferred.

The timing places the integration alongside a fast-rising, but still hard-to-interpret, tally of automated payments. The XRPL AI Hub’s live dashboard, operated by payments infrastructure developer t54, showed more than 14.5 million cumulative transactions and 170 registered merchants when checked. It also listed about 6,995 XRP and 13,117 RLUSD settled. The transaction count is therefore a measure of activity, not a proxy for equivalent dollar volume or broad consumer use.

What changes when Privy wallets are built into XRPL apps?

Developers can create and manage wallets programmatically through Privy, then use its infrastructure to sign XRPL transactions. The company says users can export their private keys or seed phrases, giving them a way to take wallet access elsewhere. For payment firms, the appeal is that customers may use stablecoins inside an existing product without separately installing and configuring wallet software.

That reduces setup work for both app builders and users, while shifting more of the wallet experience into the service provider’s product. A separate wallet can offer a more distinct boundary between an app and a customer’s assets; an embedded wallet makes the payment flow simpler, but places greater weight on how the app handles access, recovery and transaction permissions. Privy says policy controls and gas sponsorship are planned areas for deeper integration, so those capabilities should be treated as future work rather than features confirmed in the announcement.

For agents, the proposed use is more specific than general autonomous spending. A server-side wallet can hold funds and make x402 payments, a request-and-payment flow that can let software pay for online services such as data or APIs. The service can ask for payment, the agent authorizes it, and access follows once payment is verified. This can remove the need for a person to complete a checkout for every request, while still requiring a funded wallet and rules for what the agent is allowed to spend.

Does the 14 million count show meaningful payment demand?

It shows a rising number of recorded settlements, but the totals leave the economic scale open. The XRPL AI Hub’s count spans many small payments, and the XRP and RLUSD amounts it reports are modest relative to 14 million transactions. That pattern fits per-request payments for digital services; it does not establish that the transactions represent large transfers, repeat customers or widespread adoption by businesses.

Ripple has already offered developers an AI Starter Kit for XRPL payments, and its September update added support for Stripe and Tempo’s Machine Payments Protocol alongside x402. The Privy integration adds wallet infrastructure to that push, rather than replacing those payment standards. It gives developers another way to place XRPL wallet functions inside their products, while the actual payment flow still depends on the standard and service they use.

What should developers and users watch next?

The next evidence will be whether the announced tools become available in production apps, whether planned policy controls and gas sponsorship arrive, and whether transaction growth is accompanied by rising settlement amounts and a wider set of merchants. Those measures would help distinguish repeat machine payments from a high count dominated by tiny requests. For now, the partnership lowers a wallet-integration hurdle; the dashboard shows activity growing, but not yet what that activity is worth in practical payment demand.
