Samsung Wallet sets October launch for USDC transfers

Samsung plans late-October USDC transfers in Wallet for eligible Galaxy users, adding crypto and local-currency bank payouts across more than 60 countries.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 14, 2026 · 23:01 UTC#17d59d4 min read

Samsung Electronics America plans to add USDC transfers to Samsung Wallet in the last week of October, putting stablecoin transfers inside an app already used for cards and other digital items. The change brings the option to as many as 82 million U.S. Galaxy devices compatible with Samsung Wallet, though access will be limited to eligible users. In its announcement, Samsung said users will be able to send USDC to compatible crypto wallets abroad or send money to eligible bank accounts in more than 60 countries, where recipients can receive local currency.

That adds a route between two existing alternatives: using a separate crypto app and wallet, or sending a conventional international bank transfer. Wallet users will not need to install another crypto app or manage private keys themselves, Samsung says. But the two transfer routes have different costs: Samsung says it will not charge for transfers to external wallets, while bank transfers carry fees that vary by destination and amount.

What will Galaxy users be able to send, and to whom?

At launch, USDC will be the only supported stablecoin. Users can send it to compatible third-party wallets or exchange accounts on blockchain networks supported by Samsung Wallet. Samsung names Solana and Sui among the partners that will provide infrastructure and network support, but has not specified which network will handle each transfer.

The wallet will also offer transfers to eligible bank accounts in more than 60 countries. Those recipients do not need a crypto wallet: Samsung says funds can arrive in local currency. The bank route is closer to a conventional remittance, while sending USDC to an external wallet leaves the recipient with the stablecoin. Country coverage, supported banks, fees and estimated delivery times will be shown in the app before the sender confirms a bank transfer.

How does the service differ from a standalone crypto wallet?

Samsung is putting the stablecoin option in its existing Wallet interface rather than asking users to set up a separate crypto app and handle private keys. That may lower the setup burden for people who already use a Galaxy phone, while making Samsung’s software and its financial partners central to access and custody.

Samsung says Bastion will provide the stablecoin accounts and cross-border payment framework, with Coinbase acting as Bastion’s sub-custodian through Coinbase Prime Vault. Biometric authentication on registered devices will be required to initiate transfers. Samsung says it does not hold customer funds or provide the custody and money-movement services itself; those are handled by partners and subject to their terms.

The no-fee claim also has a boundary. Samsung says it will not charge for USDC transfers to external wallets, but recipient wallets or exchanges may impose their own fees, and transaction speed depends on network conditions. Bank-account transfers have separate charges. Samsung’s announcement coverage by The Block likewise reported fees on the bank payout route, underscoring that a stablecoin balance does not make every path free.

What will show whether the rollout reaches beyond Galaxy owners?

The first test is availability: Samsung says the feature is for eligible U.S. residents aged 18 or older with a Samsung Account, a compatible Galaxy device running Android 13 or later, identity verification and biometric setup. The 82 million figure describes compatible U.S. devices, not confirmed users or completed transfers. The actual country and bank list, delivery times and user take-up will show how much of that potential audience can use the service in practice.

Samsung has also described online and in-store stablecoin payments, and expansion to other markets, as possible future capabilities rather than launch features. The signals to watch are whether the October rollout arrives on schedule, how the wallet handles network selection and transfer support, and whether bank payouts prove competitive after their fees and delivery times are disclosed.
