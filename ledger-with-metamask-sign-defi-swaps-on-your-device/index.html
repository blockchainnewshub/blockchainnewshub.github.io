Ledger with MetaMask: Sign DeFi swaps on your device

How to connect a Ledger to MetaMask for DeFi swaps: update the Ethereum app, choose the right account path, and approve each transaction on your Ledger.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 24, 2026 · 05:48 UTC#8227a15 min read

A Ledger connects to MetaMask as a hardware signer for DeFi swaps. Update the device and Ethereum app in Ledger Live, close Ledger Live, then add the Ledger in MetaMask and choose the account holding your funds. Unlike a swap started in Ledger Live Discover, a DEX such as SyncSwap can request a transaction through MetaMask; the Ledger signs it on the device.

  • Choose MetaMask + Ledger when you need access to dApps on networks MetaMask supports and want swaps signed on your device.
  • Choose Ledger Live Discover when the dApp you need is available there and you prefer to start from Ledger Live.
  • Consider Rabby + Ledger when its dApp and network support fits your route and its transaction preview shows the details you need.

How do you connect a Ledger to MetaMask?

MetaMask keeps the Ledger account’s public address, while the private key stays on the Ledger. The extension prepares a transaction for the device to review and sign; it does not turn the Ledger account into a software wallet.

  1. Update Ledger firmware and install the latest Ethereum app in Ledger Live. Use a Chromium-based browser, then unlock the Ledger and open the Ethereum app.

    Ledger’s setup sequence starts with current device software and the relevant blockchain app. [Ledger’s connection steps](https://support.ledger.com/article/4404366864657-zd)

  2. Close Ledger Live before connecting MetaMask. Ledger Live and MetaMask can compete for the device connection, so leave Ledger Live closed while using the extension.
  3. In MetaMask, open the account menu, choose Add account or hardware wallet (or the equivalent hardware wallet option), and select Ledger. Follow the browser prompt to connect the device. [MetaMask’s hardware wallet flow](https://support.metamask.io/vi/more-web3/wallets/how-to-connect-a-trezor-or-ledger-hardware-wallet/)
  4. Choose a derivation path, then select the Ledger account you want to use. MetaMask offers Ledger Live, Legacy (MEW/MyCrypto), and BIP-44 options. They can produce different addresses from the same device because each path derives accounts differently. [MetaMask’s derivation path details](https://support.metamask.io/tl/configure/wallet/importing-a-seed-phrase-from-another-wallet-software-derivation-path/) [BIP-44](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0044.mediawiki)
  5. Connect to the DEX on the intended network and request a small test swap. Review the transaction details in MetaMask, then confirm that the prompt appears on the Ledger screen and approve it there. A successful device prompt and approval show that the connection works. For a swap on SyncSwap, use the network and account that hold the tokens you intend to trade.

The account path is the setup choice most likely to make a working Ledger look empty. Say you usually see your funds in Ledger Live, but choose Legacy in MetaMask: the device connects, yet the displayed addresses may not include the one holding those funds. MetaMask has not lost the balance; it is showing accounts derived along another path. Return to account selection and try the path that holds your funds before sending assets or assuming the wallet is empty.

What should you check before approving a swap?

Check the Ledger display as carefully as the MetaMask prompt. Confirm the network, recipient or contract details, token and amount, and any approval before signing. A swap can involve a token approval as well as the trade itself, so read each device prompt rather than treating the whole flow as one action.

Some contract calls or typed-data requests may require Blind signing to be enabled in the Ethereum app settings. EIP-712 is a standard for typed-data signatures, and the device may show signature information in a readable form when it can interpret the request. If the device reports that the request cannot be fully decoded, enabling Blind signing permits signing an opaque request; it does not make that request safe. Review what the screen actually shows and reject a prompt whose details you cannot verify. [Ledger’s signing details](https://developers.ledger.com/docs/device-app/integration/design-guidelines/transactions) [EIP-712](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-712)

The practical choice between MetaMask + Ledger, Ledger Live Discover, and Rabby depends on two checks: whether the route supports the dApp and network you need, and whether its preview gives enough detail to judge the transaction. MetaMask + Ledger is the flexible route when you need a network MetaMask supports. Discover and Rabby are alternatives if they support your dApp and chain; compare the transaction information they show before choosing. In each case, the device remains the approval point, but the connection path and transaction preview can differ.

Related questions

Can I use Ledger with MetaMask mobile?

Mobile pairing is a separate setup from the browser extension steps above. Check Ledger’s current instructions for your device and phone before choosing this route.

Why is my Ledger not detected by MetaMask?

First close Ledger Live and other apps that may be using the device. Confirm the Ledger is unlocked, the Ethereum app is open, and the browser supports the connection method; Ledger identifies Chromium-based browsers such as Chrome and Brave as desktop options.

Does a Ledger work on zkSync Era through MetaMask?

Use MetaMask + Ledger when the network and dApp are supported by MetaMask, then check that the Ledger account is on the intended network and the transaction can be reviewed on-device. Network support and device signing are separate checks.

Before approving a swap, compare the amount and address on the Ledger screen with the action you intended. That quick review can catch a wrong account, token approval, or request you did not mean to sign.
