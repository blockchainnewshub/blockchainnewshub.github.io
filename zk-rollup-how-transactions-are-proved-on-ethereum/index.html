ZK rollup: How transactions are proved on Ethereum

What is a zk rollup and how does it work in simple terms? It runs transactions off Ethereum and posts a validity proof so Ethereum can verify the result.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 27, 2026 · 22:58 UTC#4bb3ad4 min read

A zk rollup runs transactions away from Ethereum, then posts their data and a cryptographic proof so Ethereum can accept the updated state. That changes how the work is done: Ethereum checks a proof instead of repeating every transaction, while an optimistic rollup like Base relies on a challenge period.

What does a zk rollup do?

A zk rollup groups transactions made on its own network and settles their result on Ethereum. Users can swap assets there; an operator collects those transactions into a batch, and a prover creates one validity proof for the batch.

The proof attests that the transactions were processed according to the rollup’s rules. Ethereum’s verifier contract checks it, and, if it passes, accepts the new state root: a compact summary of the rollup’s account balances and other state. The batch’s data is also posted to Ethereum, so the result is tied to information available on the base chain.

For the related cross-chain step, the Base bridge covers how supported assets move between Ethereum and Base. Base is an optimistic rollup, so withdrawals through its bridge can face a challenge period of about seven days. A zk rollup uses proof verification instead of that waiting window.

How does a zk rollup process a batch?

A zk rollup takes transactions from users, executes them off Ethereum, and submits data and a proof for Ethereum to check. Follow one batch: several users swap tokens on the rollup. The operator bundles their transactions, and the rollup executes them to calculate the resulting balances.

Next, a prover generates a single validity proof for the batch. The proof is a cryptographic statement that the execution followed the network’s rules and produced the claimed result. It can use a SNARK or a STARK; both are types of validity proof, though their technical designs differ.

The operator posts the batch data and proof to Ethereum. A verifier contract checks the proof. If it is valid, Ethereum accepts the new state root, and the batch’s outcome becomes part of the rollup’s settled state. Funds can then leave the rollup once the proof has been verified and the relevant withdrawal steps are complete.

This is the key difference from simply trusting an operator’s claim. Ethereum’s contract checks cryptographic evidence before accepting the updated state. The proof lets Ethereum verify the result without re-running all the swaps itself.

Why can zk rollup fees and withdrawals be lower or faster?

A zk rollup can reduce fees per transaction because the cost of posting and verifying a batch is shared across its transactions. Ethereum checks one proof for the batch rather than executing each rollup transaction again. The more transactions included, the more that shared work can spread across them, although actual fees depend on the network and its activity.

Withdrawals can also avoid the fixed challenge window used by optimistic rollups. An optimistic rollup assumes a submitted batch is valid and allows time for challenges. With a zk rollup, the validity proof is checked before Ethereum accepts the new state, so there is no equivalent dispute period to wait through before the state is accepted.

The withdrawal wait on a zk rollup depends on how often its operator generates and posts proofs. If proofs are produced and verified promptly, users may be able to withdraw sooner; if proof submission follows a slower schedule, the wait can be longer. That makes the network’s proof schedule a practical detail to check when timing a withdrawal.

The trade-off is that proof generation and verification are part of the system’s operation. Users benefit from Ethereum checking the result without re-executing every transaction, while the rollup depends on its operator and prover to produce and submit proofs. The schedule therefore matters alongside the headline claim that withdrawals are faster.

Which networks use zk rollups, and what should you watch?

zkSync Era, Starknet, Linea, and Scroll are zk rollups. They use validity proofs to have Ethereum verify rollup state updates, while Base is an optimistic rollup on the OP Stack and uses a challenge period for withdrawals.

In practice, people use rollups to make transactions such as swaps without having every step run directly on Ethereum. When choosing a network for a transfer or trade, identify whether it is a zk or optimistic rollup, then check how its withdrawal process works. The label tells you which verification approach it uses; the proof posting schedule helps you estimate when funds may be ready to leave.

You can now explain the core distinction: a zk rollup executes transactions off Ethereum and proves the batch correct, while an optimistic rollup allows time for challenges. For a withdrawal, check the network’s proof schedule or challenge window before deciding when to move funds.
