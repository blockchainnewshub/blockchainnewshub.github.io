EU’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal puts crypto in focus

The Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal would cut 60 banks from SWIFT and widen crypto scrutiny, but needs unanimous EU approval to take effect.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 26, 2026 · 21:33 UTC#aaa4ab3 min read

The European Commission’s proposed 22nd sanctions package would extend the transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks, remove them from SWIFT and tighten scrutiny of crypto and payment networks. In its Nov. 23 statement, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the measures would also cover third countries helping Russia evade sanctions and accompany a newly launched Crypto Sanctions Task Force. The package remains a proposal: as the Associated Press reported, all 27 EU members must endorse it, a process that can take weeks and may change the measures.

The crypto provisions broaden the focus from restricting named services to addressing networks that may help Russian entities get around existing restrictions. Von der Leyen’s statement does not identify platforms, countries or specific transaction types. It says the package will target crypto and payment networks in Russia and in third countries that help evade sanctions, with the task force intended to support that effort. The approach could widen the reach of enforcement; its practical effect will depend on how the measures are defined and applied across jurisdictions.

How the proposal compares with the July package

The EU adopted its 21st sanctions package in July. The Commission’s account of that package says it imposed transaction bans on more than 100 Russian banks in total and introduced a dedicated third-country ban for crypto-asset services, alongside transaction bans on additional third-country crypto platforms and crypto-linked firms. The new proposal’s roughly 60 banks are an additional target group, not a replacement for that wider existing total. The measures also differ in emphasis: the July package set out specific crypto-service restrictions, while von der Leyen’s announcement describes a broader focus on crypto and payment networks and evasion, without giving the proposed platform list.

That distinction matters for businesses and users who rely on intermediaries. Expanding restrictions to networks outside Russia may make it harder to route payments through third countries, but it also leaves more detail to be settled in the final legal text. Until the package is adopted and its scope published, the announcement alone does not tell service providers which platforms or transactions will be affected.

  • About 60 banks: The Commission proposes extending the transaction ban and removing all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments. The proposal does not say that all cross-border payments involving Russia would stop.
  • Crypto and payment networks: The stated focus includes third countries helping Russia evade sanctions. The task force adds an enforcement mechanism, though the announcement gives no public list of targets.
  • More than 70 ships: The Commission proposes listing additional vessels from Russia’s shadow fleet and banning tankers insured in Russia. It also proposes extending the energy-services ban to electricity services.
  • Sports and culture: Russian teams and athletes would be barred from competing under the Russian flag at EU events; official symbols, sponsor funding and Russian participation in federation governing bodies would also face restrictions.

The package reaches beyond finance: it also proposes visa restrictions for Russian ex-combatants and a ban on Europeans holding posts in the governing bodies of companies in Russia. That breadth adds pressure across finance, energy, travel and public events, while making agreement among 27 governments a larger part of the path to implementation.

The next signals are whether member states approve the package, whether its crypto measures retain the proposed third-country reach, and what platforms and payment activity the final text covers. Until then, the 60-bank figure and the wider crypto push describe proposed measures, not rules already in force.
