Binance publishes verifiable reserves after $6.3bn breach

Binance says it has replenished 95% of the assets lost in its $6.3bn breach and published verifiable backing for ten tokens, while recovery continues.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 2, 2026 · 13:25 UTC#5d64a74 min read

Binance says users can now verify that ten major tokens are backed one-to-one after the exchange’s hot wallets were compromised and about $6.3 billion in assets were stolen. The update marks a shift from emergency containment toward proving customer balances and restoring access, but the exchange says 5% of the affected assets remain to be sourced. Its latest recovery update says the proof covers BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD and ZEC.

The scale is far beyond the $1.5 billion Bybit theft in 2025, which the Associated Press describes as the previous largest crypto exchange hack. Binance’s Oct. 30 incident update says unauthorized withdrawals began around 20:05 UTC the day before, after hot wallets were compromised. Binance has said an initial investigation pointed to a potentially compromised laptop, then identified additional points of breach; it has not published a full account of how the attacker gained access.

Did Binance halt trading, and what was actually suspended?

Binance’s statements describe a temporary suspension of withdrawals, not a halt to trading. The exchange said deposits and other unaffected services remained operational while it reviewed security, contained the threat and checked affected systems. That distinction matters: users could continue using services that remained available, but they could not freely move assets off the platform during the suspension.

On Nov. 1, Binance began restoring withdrawals in phases, starting with selected assets across networks including Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Tron and Solana. The company said the rollout prioritized assets according to recent user demand and would expand as readiness checks were completed. This staged approach gives the exchange more time to test systems and manage risk; it also means access may return at different times for different assets and networks.

Reuters reported that Binance and its partners had frozen about $3 billion in affected assets by Oct. 30. Binance has pledged to absorb the financial impact rather than pass the loss to users. According to the exchange’s Nov. 2 update, it has now replenished about 95% of the impacted amount. That figure is Binance’s own report; the outstanding 5% consists of tokens outside the ten covered by the latest proof, which the company says are less liquid and take longer to acquire at scale.

What does the new proof let customers check?

Binance says the proof for its Oct. 31 reserves snapshot is now available for users to verify. The exchange uses a Merkle tree and a zero-knowledge proof, which it says lets a user check that their own balance is included and backed at a minimum of one-to-one without disclosing individual balances. That gives customers a way to check the stated backing for the ten named tokens rather than rely only on the company’s public assurance.

The proof has a narrower reach than a full account of the breach or a guarantee about every asset and service. It covers the listed tokens at the snapshot time, while Binance says it is still acquiring the remaining affected assets and plans broader token coverage in a later snapshot. A reserve check also does not explain how the attacker entered the systems or whether the security changes will prevent a similar breach.

Which recovery signals come next?

Binance says another reserves snapshot, covering more tokens, is planned for Nov. 4; generating its full proof is expected to take about 40 to 50 hours. It also says the next phase of withdrawal restoration is planned for Nov. 3, with details to follow. The exchange has promised a post-incident report after the investigation is complete.

The key tests are whether that wider proof confirms coverage beyond the first ten tokens, whether the remaining assets are replenished, and whether withdrawals return across networks without fresh interruptions. The record size of the loss makes Binance’s funding commitment consequential, while the staged reopening and incomplete technical account leave customers with practical questions still to resolve.
