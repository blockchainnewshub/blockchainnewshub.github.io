Dogecoin Labs joins MyDoge, DogeOS and Such App under one roof

Dogecoin Labs will combine MyDoge, DogeOS and Such App, linking a wallet, a developer testnet and merchant tools while leaving adoption and integration unproven.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 27, 2026 · 09:12 UTC#a8e3d63 min read

Dogecoin Labs announced on Oct. 21 that it will bring MyDoge, DogeOS and Such App under one independent organization, linking a DOGE wallet, an application platform and merchant payment tools that had been developed separately. The DogeOS announcement says the initiative was formed in partnership with the Dogecoin Foundation and will be led by MyDoge and DogeOS founder Jordan Jefferson. The change joins the pieces of a proposed DOGE economy in one organization; it does not itself make the products fully integrated or change Dogecoin’s underlying network.

The arrangement pairs MyDoge’s consumer wallet with DogeOS’s developer environment and Such App’s merchant tools. That gives the project a more direct route from building an application to putting it in front of holders and accepting DOGE for goods or services. Previously, the products and teams sat across separate organizations. The Foundation says it will continue its stewardship, open-source development support, advocacy and community work, while Labs focuses on products and commercial opportunities.

What does each product add to the new organization?

MyDoge supplies the wallet and consumer access, DogeOS the application infrastructure, and Such App the merchant payment tools. MyDoge says it has more than 500,000 users and recently rolled out its V3 update, which connects the wallet with applications being built on DogeOS. That figure is a reported user total, not a measure of how many people are active or using those applications.

DogeOS opened a public testnet on Sept. 30, according to the announcement. Its EVM-compatible environment lets developers use familiar tools to build and test applications. Such App’s beta already supports invoicing and in-person QR payments for goods and services in DOGE. Its integration with DogeOS is planned, rather than complete. CoinDesk’s report likewise describes the three products as a potential chain from developers to wallet users to merchants, while noting that the payment app’s connection to DogeOS remains in planning.

How much changes for DOGE holders and developers now?

The immediate change is organizational: product, development and commercial capabilities are being consolidated, while the Foundation retains its ecosystem role. For developers, a shared organization could connect app development with an existing wallet audience. For holders, the intended gain is more applications and places to spend DOGE. Compared with relying on Dogecoin’s base network for transfers alone, DogeOS is meant to provide a separate application environment; that adds room for app development, but also makes delivery and integration dependent on a newer platform.

That trade-off is visible in the products’ current stages. DogeOS is on testnet, not described as a production launch, and Such App’s DogeOS integration remains planned. The announcements give no date for either integration or a DogeOS mainnet. MyDoge’s user count offers a starting audience, but it does not establish that wallet holders will try apps or spend DOGE through merchants.

What should readers watch for next?

The practical measures are whether DogeOS moves beyond testnet, whether its applications become available to wallet users, and whether Such App completes its integration and expands beyond beta payment functions. Merchant activity and evidence that holders use the services would show whether putting the teams together produces more than shared ownership. For now, Dogecoin Labs has assembled the wallet, developer layer and payment tools; the scale of DOGE use those products can support remains to be demonstrated.
