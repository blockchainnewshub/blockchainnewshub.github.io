OCC leaves stablecoin rewards open to case-by-case review

The OCC finalized its GENIUS Act rule without barring exchange rewards, but left programs to case-by-case review while issuers remain barred from paying yield.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 22, 2026 · 15:13 UTC#205c333 min read

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency finalized its first major rule under the GENIUS Act on Oct. 21, leaving crypto exchanges able to offer stablecoin rewards subject to case-by-case scrutiny while keeping the ban on issuers paying holders yield. The [Associated Press report on the rule](https://apnews.com/article/stablecoins-circle-usdc-banks-interest-cryptocurrencies-db6b787c7c2a5fe434ce84b23413d966) describes the practical split: issuers such as Circle cannot pay interest directly to coin holders, while platforms such as Coinbase and Robinhood may pass along earnings as rewards.

That leaves the basic distinction in place, but not a blanket safe harbor for exchange programs. The OCC’s [final rule bulletin](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-50.html) implements the stablecoin law’s limits for firms under the agency’s jurisdiction; the rule takes effect Jan. 18, 2027.

What does the rule allow exchanges to do?

It leaves room for rewards that are not paid “solely” because a customer holds, uses or retains a payment stablecoin. The OCC said it would assess arrangements against the law’s language and the facts of each case, according to CoinDesk’s [account of the final rule](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/21/occ-s-final-rule-to-start-fresh-in-weighing-stablecoin-rewards-against-genius-act-ban). A reward tied at least partly to another reason may fall outside the prohibition, but the agency did not specify a general test or list of permitted structures.

That uncertainty matters for existing programs. The OCC declined to presume that programs already operating before the GENIUS Act comply with the new law, and they receive no special treatment simply because they predate it. The final rule also removed proposed “rebuttable presumptions” that would have treated specified activities as violations unless firms proved otherwise. That gives exchanges room to make their case, while leaving the regulator discretion to challenge the details.

How is this different from the issuer ban?

The law bars a permitted payment stablecoin issuer from paying holders interest or yield simply for holding, using or retaining the coin. The final rule maintains that boundary. Exchanges and other platforms sit on the other side of it: they may offer rewards, but a program that functions as a way to route issuer yield directly to holders could attract scrutiny under the case-by-case approach.

The distinction matters because stablecoins are backed by assets such as bank deposits and U.S. Treasury securities that can earn interest. The crypto industry has sought to share some of that return with customers; banks have argued that higher crypto yields could draw deposits away from lenders. AP reported that Coinbase offered up to 3.5% on USDC balances, compared with a 0.38% average savings yield cited from FDIC figures. That spread shows the attraction for customers, but the comparison does not make the products equivalent: stablecoin balances do not have the same federal deposit insurance as bank accounts.

What should platforms and banks watch next?

For exchanges, the next test is how the OCC applies the “solely” standard to specific reward designs once the rule takes effect. The agency will monitor the industry and says it may adjust its supervisory framework as it develops. For banks, the final rule preserves a route for crypto platforms to compete for customer funds, though it does not settle how much deposit movement that competition will cause.

The OCC rule covers its part of GENIUS Act implementation; Treasury, the Federal Reserve and the FDIC have separate work underway. Their rules, along with any OCC decisions on individual programs, will show whether rewards remain a meaningful customer incentive or become harder to offer without a separate activity beyond holding a stablecoin.
