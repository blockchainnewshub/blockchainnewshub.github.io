How to bridge tokens with lifi bridge and confirm arrival

To bridge tokens with lifi bridge, connect your wallet, compare the amount received, approve and sign the transfer, then track its source hash until tokens arrive.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 05:24 UTC#daa3275 min read

The lifi bridge lets you move tokens between EVM chains by connecting a wallet, choosing a route, approving and signing, then tracking the transfer until the tokens arrive. It aggregates bridges such as Across, Stargate and Relay, along with decentralized exchanges, into one quote, so you choose among routes instead of comparing each bridge separately.

That saves you from selecting a bridge and swap venue one at a time, while the route still determines the amount received and how long the transfer takes. For a single transfer, use the lifi bridge when you want a LI.FI cross-chain swap; treat the quoted amount as the main comparison and keep the source chain’s gas coin ready before you start.

How to use the lifi bridge for one transfer

To bridge tokens with lifi bridge, start with tokens in a self-custody wallet on an EVM source chain and a gas coin on that chain. The gas coin pays for wallet transactions, so the transfer cannot proceed through its signing prompts unless that balance is available. You’re ready when the wallet shows the source tokens and enough gas coin to submit transactions.

  1. Open a LI.FI bridge front end. Connect the self-custody wallet you’ll use for the transfer. LI.FI Widget is another LI.FI interface name you may encounter; Jumper is another name in this cross-chain landscape.
  2. Set the source chain, token and amount. Choose the chain where your tokens are held, then select the token and enter how much to send. Check that the wallet is connected to that same source chain before continuing.
  3. Set the destination chain and token. Choose the EVM chain where you want the funds to land and the token you want to receive. Confirm the destination token is the one you intend to hold there.
  4. Choose a route. Compare the quoted amount received and the estimated time. Routes can differ on both, so choose based on the amount you’ll receive, with timing as a trade-off to consider. A route may combine a bridge such as Across, Stargate or Relay with a swap.
  5. Approve the token, if prompted. An ERC-20 token needs an approval transaction before the transfer can use it. This is a separate wallet prompt from the transfer itself: review and sign the approval, then continue when it is complete. Native coins do not need an ERC-20 approval.
  6. Sign the transfer. Review the transfer details and sign the transaction in your wallet. This sends the source-chain transaction that starts the route. Keep the transaction hash shown after signing; it identifies that source transaction.
  7. Track the transfer and check the destination balance. Paste the source-chain transaction hash into LI.FI Explorer. Follow the transfer there until its status reads done, and note the destination hash shown for the second leg. Then switch your wallet to the destination network and confirm the token balance appears there. A wallet left on the source network hides the arrived balance from view.

Why the source hash matters

The source-chain hash is the handoff between signing and confirming arrival. The route has a source transaction and a destination leg; the source hash lets LI.FI Explorer look up the transfer and follow both legs. When the status reads done, the destination hash gives you the transaction record for the destination leg.

That is more useful than treating the first signed transaction as proof that the whole transfer has finished. Keep the source hash until the transfer completes, then use the destination network in your wallet to verify the result. If the Explorer says done but the balance is not visible, check that the wallet is set to the destination chain and that you are viewing the selected destination token.

Can I send bridged tokens to a different address?

A different recipient is possible only when the front end and selected route support sending to one. This walkthrough assumes the connected wallet receives the tokens. If you intend to use another address, confirm the recipient and destination details before signing, since a completed transfer goes to the address specified for it.

What is the minimum amount worth bridging?

There is no single amount that makes sense for every transfer. Compare the amount received with the amount sent and decide whether the result is useful for your purpose; routes can return different amounts. If the quoted output is too small for what you need, don’t sign that transfer.

For how to use LI.FI for bridging, the useful signals are the selected route’s output, the source hash, a done status in LI.FI Explorer and the destination balance. The lifi bridge combines route selection with the transfer flow; your wallet signatures and the final balance check remain the points where you confirm what is happening.

  • Confirm the source tokens and gas coin are in your wallet.
  • Choose the destination chain and token, then compare route amounts.
  • Approve the ERC-20 token if prompted, then sign the transfer.
  • Track the source hash in LI.FI Explorer until done.
  • Switch to the destination network and confirm the balance.
