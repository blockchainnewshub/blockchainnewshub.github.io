Moody’s and Chainlink test multi-chain ratings delivery

Moody’s and Chainlink tested sending credit insights across multiple blockchains, extending beyond a single network but stopping short of live onchain ratings.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 11, 2026 · 09:36 UTC#9c7ba13 min read

Moody’s Ratings and Chainlink have completed a testnet pilot for distributing credit insights across multiple blockchains at once, extending Moody’s tokenized finance work beyond a single network while stopping short of publishing live ratings onchain. The companies said on Nov. 10 that Moody’s Token Integration Engine (TIE) worked with Chainlink’s Cross-Chain Interoperability Protocol (CCIP) to send representative test data to permissioned and permissionless networks. Chainlink’s announcement on the pilot says the exercise did not involve actual rating actions or live dissemination.

How does the pilot extend Moody’s earlier blockchain work?

The test shifts the focus from making credit analysis available on a blockchain to distributing it across several networks. The Block reported that Moody’s earlier token-engine deployment began on Canton; this pilot tested simultaneous delivery to multiple permissioned and permissionless networks. The announcement does not identify the networks involved, so it does not establish which chains or tokenized assets could use the system in a production setting.

The mechanism joins Moody’s TIE, designed to disseminate ratings and related data in blockchain environments, with CCIP for cross-chain delivery. Moody’s says the test preserved its existing publication and governance processes. That makes the proposal different from treating each blockchain as a separate destination for credit information, while leaving Moody’s established processes in place for producing and publishing its opinions.

The scale of Moody’s existing digital-asset activity provides context, but not evidence of adoption of this system: the company says it rated or assessed more than 70 digital issuances, including digital bonds and tokenized funds, between January 2017 and October 2026. The Block’s report also describes the pilot as an expansion from the Canton deployment to multi-chain distribution.

What did the test demonstrate, and what remains unproven?

It demonstrated technical distribution using representative data in a controlled testnet environment, according to the companies. It did not show live ratings moving across networks, how rating changes would appear to users, or whether issuers and investors will use the workflow. Those limits matter: a successful test of delivery is a step toward broader access, but it does not establish production reliability or change how Moody’s reaches its credit opinions.

The multi-chain model could make credit information available across tokenized markets built on different networks. Compared with a single-network deployment, it is aimed at broader reach; the corresponding challenge is keeping information and controls consistent across more than one environment. The announcement says the model is designed to accommodate future updates, including rating actions, while maintaining governance and lifecycle controls. It gives no production timetable, named network list, performance figures or commercial terms.

What should readers watch next?

The next meaningful signal is whether the companies move from representative test data to a production workflow, and on which networks. Any launch would need to show how updates are handled while Moody’s publication process remains authoritative. Investors should also watch for evidence of actual use by tokenized asset platforms: without live dissemination or named integrations, the pilot establishes a tested route for distribution, not a new source of onchain ratings or a change in credit coverage.
