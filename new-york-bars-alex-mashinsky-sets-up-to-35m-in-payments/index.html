New York Bars Alex Mashinsky, Sets Up to $35M in Payments

New York’s settlement bars Alex Mashinsky from crypto and sets up to $35 million in conditional payments, adding a state remedy to his federal prison sentence.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 12, 2026 · 22:25 UTC#b80d1f4 min read

New York Attorney General Letitia James has secured a permanent industry ban and up to $35 million in conditional payments from Alex Mashinsky, adding a state civil remedy to the 12-year federal prison sentence he is already serving. The agreement resolves New York’s 2023 lawsuit over claims that the Celsius co-founder misled customers about the crypto lender’s safety. In its October 9 announcement, the attorney general’s office said the ban covers the securities, commodities and cryptocurrency industries. The headline figure is not an immediate $35 million payment: the state’s recovery depends on conditions tied to Mashinsky’s federal forfeiture and prison sentence.

How could the state collect the $35 million?

The settlement creates two separate payment triggers, according to the attorney general’s office. Mashinsky must pay New York $25 million if he fails to forfeit $10 million in ill-gotten gains to the federal government under his federal plea agreement. He must pay another $10 million if he does not serve his full prison sentence, as mandated by the criminal court and overseen by the Bureau of Prisons. The amounts add up to a maximum of $35 million, but the announcement describes them as contingent obligations rather than money already collected.

That structure makes the agreement different from a straightforward fine imposed for a new criminal conviction. Mashinsky is serving 12 years in federal prison after a separate criminal case, and the New York settlement resolves the state’s civil claims. He was also ordered to forfeit more than $48 million to the federal government in that case, the attorney general’s office said. The state’s $25 million condition refers to the separate $10 million forfeiture requirement in his plea agreement, in addition to assets already forfeited.

What does the New York ban add to earlier penalties?

The permanent ban bars Mashinsky from participating in securities, commodities and cryptocurrency industries. CoinDesk reported on the settlement that he had already been permanently barred from commodities activity by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission in June. The New York order therefore extends the restrictions across additional financial and crypto activity, while overlapping with the existing commodities prohibition.

The remedy also sits alongside, rather than replacing, the recovery process for Celsius customers. More than $3.4 billion had been distributed to creditors in Celsius’ bankruptcy as of August 2026, according to the attorney general. Separately, Celsius founders and executives were forced to pay $16.5 million to the Federal Trade Commission. Those figures describe different proceedings and recipients: the New York settlement’s conditional payments are owed to the state, while bankruptcy distributions went to creditors.

What remains to be resolved for Celsius customers?

The state’s case focused on Mashinsky’s representations about Celsius, not on a new distribution plan for creditors. James’s office said he encouraged deposits by misleading investors about the platform’s safety, user numbers and investment strategies; it said Celsius used customer assets in high-risk strategies and concealed losses. Celsius had offered customers high yields on deposited crypto, but, unlike banks, it was not subject to the same strict federal and state requirements, according to the attorney general’s account.

The trade-off in the settlement is clear: New York secured a broad, permanent industry bar and potential financial penalties, but collection of the full amount depends on conditions rather than an immediate payment. For customers, the reported $3.4 billion in bankruptcy distributions remains a separate measure of recovery and does not establish that every creditor has been made whole. The next signals are whether Mashinsky fulfills the separate $10 million federal forfeiture requirement, whether he serves the full sentence, and how the bankruptcy process accounts for any remaining creditor claims.
