Binance reopens withdrawals after $6.3bn hack

Binance has begun restoring withdrawals after a record $6.3 billion hack, while the FBI attributes the theft to North Korea and tracing efforts continue.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 1, 2026 · 13:10 UTC#0930ec3 min read

Binance began restoring withdrawals in phases on Sunday, four days after a reported $6.3 billion theft led it to suspend withdrawals while it checked the platform’s security. The FBI has since attributed the attack to North Korea, raising the stakes from a record exchange breach to an active effort to stop the stolen crypto being laundered. Binance’s suspension covered deposits and withdrawals; reports do not confirm that trading itself was halted.

According to the CoinDesk report on Binance’s phased withdrawal restart, the first window opened at 06:00 UTC on Sunday, with bitcoin and assets across 15 other blockchain networks included. Binance says further access will depend on security and stability checks for each network. The exchange’s withdrawal recovery update says deposits remained open during the suspension.

How does this compare with the previous record?

The reported $6.3 billion loss is more than four times the roughly $1.5 billion stolen from Bybit in 2025, which the Associated Press identified as the previous comparison point. The FBI said the Binance theft took place on or about October 29 and named the North Korean activity TraderTraitor. It reported that the attackers were converting assets primarily into ether, with DeFi and instant swap services likely next steps. The FBI is urging exchanges, node operators and other crypto services to block transactions tied to listed addresses.

That attribution is a shift from the first hours after the incident, when blockchain firms suspected North Korean involvement but the attacker’s identity had not been confirmed by US authorities. The FBI’s assessment gives platforms and investigators a specific set of addresses and laundering routes to monitor. It does not mean the stolen assets have been recovered: tracing and blocking transfers can disrupt conversion, while assets moved beyond the reach of an issuer’s controls may be harder to freeze.

What does the phased restart restore?

Binance’s first phase includes withdrawals for bitcoin, ether, USDT, USDC, BNB, Solana, XRP and assets on networks including Tron, Arbitrum, Base and Optimism. It says it is prioritising networks and assets based on recent user demand, then reopening them as checks are completed. That sequence gives the exchange more time to test withdrawal infrastructure and watch for residual risk, but it leaves users with uneven access: an asset’s availability depends on its network clearing checks, and Binance warns that early withdrawals may face delays.

The scale of the breach makes Binance’s ability to absorb losses central to the restart. The exchange says it has replenished about 95% of the affected amount with company resources, up from 90% in its prior update, and that users will not bear the shortfall. It says its listed major balances are fully backed, while the remaining 5% consists of less liquid tokens that take longer to source. Those are Binance’s claims; the promised post-incident report and updated proof-of-reserves information will offer further evidence.

What should users and investigators watch next?

The immediate tests are whether Binance can reopen the remaining withdrawal networks without new security problems, whether the remaining assets are replenished, and whether investigators or industry partners freeze more of the stolen funds. The FBI’s warning points to ether conversions and DeFi routes as key signals. A successful restart would restore access in stages; it would not, on its own, settle how the breach happened or how much of the theft can be recovered.
