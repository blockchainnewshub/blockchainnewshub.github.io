Bridge aggregators: why a supported chain can still have no route

Why bridge aggregators support some chains but not others comes down to deployed bridges, token liquidity and the chain pairs each route can serve.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 05:41 UTC#b0cb004 min read

Bridge aggregators support only the chains and routes their integrated bridges can serve. A chain may be available for one transfer but absent for another, because coverage depends on the bridge, token and destination together.

Arbitrum-to-Solana transfers show how that works: lifi bridge can route between them through bridges such as Mayan. A smaller, newer chain may have no route because none of the bridges in an aggregator’s set connects it to the requested destination and token.

Why does coverage vary by chain, pair and token?

Bridge aggregators find routes across services; the underlying bridges move the funds. Each bridge chooses which chains to deploy on, so the aggregator’s potential coverage starts with the networks those bridges support. Adding a chain to an aggregator’s interface cannot create a bridge deployment or a transfer path that the underlying services do not provide.

That is the first difference to keep in mind when comparing providers. Across, Stargate, Mayan and deBridge are separate bridge systems an aggregator may integrate. Stargate is built on LayerZero messaging, but LayerZero messaging and an available route are not the same thing: the relevant bridge still needs deployments and a workable path for the transfer.

Even when both chains are supported, a route depends on liquidity for the token on each end. A bridge needs a way to deliver the requested asset at the destination, whether by transferring that asset or using a route that swaps into it. If liquidity is missing or too thin for the token and pair, the route may be unavailable. New or less-used chains can take time to attract the pools and bridge activity that make more routes possible.

This is why support is not a simple yes-or-no property of a chain. A chain can be listed while a particular pair has no route, or while a particular token cannot be moved across that pair. The practical coverage belongs to the combination: source chain, destination chain, token and available bridge path.

  • Bridge deployments: Each integrated bridge operates on selected chains, which sets the networks an aggregator can potentially connect.
  • Token liquidity: Funds need a viable path on both ends; scarce liquidity can remove a token route even when the chains themselves are supported.
  • Chain-pair availability: A bridge may connect a chain to some destinations but not to the one the reader selected.
  • Wallet and signing support: Non-EVM chains can require different address handling, signing and transaction indexing, so an aggregator must build support for those systems as well.

The EVM-to-Solana case adds that last complication. EVM addresses commonly use the 0x format, while Solana addresses use base58. The wallet interaction and transaction data also differ, so an aggregator needs infrastructure that can interpret and sign for both sides. Mayan can provide a bridge route between Solana and EVM chains, including the Arbitrum-to-Solana case; a new chain without a comparable integrated path may return no route.

For a newcomer, the first check is direct: choose the source chain, destination chain and token in lifi bridge. If the result says “no route,” that points to an unsupported pair or token path, rather than proving that the wallet is broken. Try another token only if you are willing to receive a different asset, and compare the result with the bridge providers’ own current information before moving funds.

Related questions

How do I check which chains lifi bridge supports?

Check the exact transfer you need by selecting both chains and the token. A general supported-chain reference can establish whether a network is in scope, but only a route search for your pair and asset can show whether that transfer is available now.

What can I do if no aggregator supports my chain?

Look for a bridge that explicitly supports both your source and destination, then confirm that it supports the token you plan to move. If no direct path exists, the alternatives are to use a supported intermediate chain or wait for a bridge deployment and liquidity to arrive; each extra transfer adds another step to verify.

Questions readers ask next

Is it safe to use a single bridge directly when the aggregator lacks my chain?

A direct bridge can be a reasonable option when it clearly supports your exact chain pair and token, but the aggregator’s missing route does not establish that a direct bridge is safe. Verify the provider and destination details through its official channels, understand the transaction you are signing, and account for the bridge’s own risks before sending funds.

How long does a new chain usually take to appear in aggregators?

There is no fixed timetable: a chain appears when bridge deployments, liquidity, wallet and signing support, and aggregator integration are in place. Watch for those concrete signals rather than assuming that a chain’s launch automatically makes it routable.
