Monad Bridge vs Stargate: Verifiers and Worst-Case Loss

A meaningful Ethereum–Monad transfer makes Monad bridge versus Stargate a route-specific safety choice: neither is safe by default. For a supported asset, use Monad bridge as your next move once its controls meet your threshold, because the Monad cross-chain bridge transfers assets between Ethereum and Monad. Both routes rely on off-chain verification; Ethereum consensus alone cannot establish that a transfer should execute on Monad.

monad-bridge.org transfers supported assets between Ethereum and Monad. For the transfer it handles, identify the exact token contract you will receive before comparing it with a Stargate route.

Monad bridge and Stargate allocate verification and loss differently

Monad bridge and Stargate put trust in different verifiers and token contracts. Weigh these four points for the same asset and Ethereum–Monad pathway:

  • Verifier set: The native bridge’s documented NTT route requires Wormhole and Axelar attestations in a 2-of-2 setup. Wormhole’s Guardian network has 19 members and requires 13 signatures for its attestation. Stargate runs on LayerZero, where each application pathway specifies its required decentralized verifier networks, or DVNs. Inspect the required DVNs for the message on LayerZero Scan. Monad’s token and bridge documentation identifies the NTT and Stargate asset routes; LayerZero’s security documentation explains pathway thresholds.
  • Token model: A pool route releases liquidity already held on the destination chain. A mint route creates destination tokens against assets locked or burned elsewhere. Stargate supports token routes as well as pools, so determine which model the quoted transfer actually uses.
  • Admin control: Inspect the source and destination contracts’ upgrade owners, pause authority and mint permissions. Record the multisig threshold or timelock where one applies. A pause key can limit an attack’s spread, but it can also leave a legitimate transfer waiting.
  • Worst-case loss: A forged release on a pool route can drain available liquidity, putting liquidity providers and pending claims at risk. A forged mint can create unbacked tokens on Monad. That dilutes every holder’s claim on the backing, including holders who never bridged.

Take a token you intend to hold for months. A mint-route breach can leave your balance unchanged while increasing the number of claims against the same backing. Selling the unbacked units can then pressure the token’s market price. A pool breach concentrates the immediate shortfall in the pool, although users awaiting settlement can still be exposed. Compare these outcomes before treating a cheaper fee or faster expected arrival as the deciding factor.

Choose the native bridge when its supported asset is the token you want to hold and you trust its verifier and mint controls. Choose Stargate when its exact pathway has acceptable DVNs and, for a quick round trip, a deeper verified pool. For a meaningful long-term holding, give mint authority more weight than pool depth: the constraint is whether the token on Monad remains fully backed.

Related bridge security comparisons

Monad bridge vs portal bridge: which is safer?

Portal Bridge is another Wormhole-based example, but shared messaging does not make its token contract or custody identical to the native NTT route. Compare the destination token address, mint permissions and backing for the asset you plan to keep.

Is Stargate audited?

Stargate publishes audit reports. Match a report’s contracts and version to your route, then inspect the current DVN configuration and admin keys. An audit covers specified code; your transfer also depends on its live configuration.

Practical questions before sending

Does Stargate support MON transfers?

MON is Monad’s gas token, and Monad is EVM-compatible. Stargate support depends on the current asset and pathway: look for MON on the Ethereum–Monad route and verify whether the destination is native MON or a token contract. Do not infer support from Stargate’s general Monad coverage.

How do I track a Stargate transfer on LayerZero Scan?

Search LayerZero Scan with the source transaction hash. Open its message, compare the required DVNs with those marked verified, and follow the destination transaction once execution appears. A missing message may mean you used a different messaging route.

What happens to funds in flight if a bridge is paused?

A pause can stop a destination release or mint after the source transaction has completed. Keep the source hash, check whether a destination transaction exists, and inspect the route’s status before sending again. Recovery depends on the affected contracts and the authority that can resume them.
