US wins court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department has court authority over more than $1.2 billion of Binance theft proceeds, while partners have frozen $2.7 billion; recovery remains unresolved.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 19, 2026 · 12:25 UTC#e248a93 min read

U.S. authorities have obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step beyond the broader freeze of more than $2.7 billion by government and private-sector partners. The Justice Department’s Nov. 12 announcement says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing; the court authorization does not mean all the funds have been finally forfeited or returned.

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. According to the Justice Department, the theft took place on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The Associated Press described the haul as about $6.3 billion and reported the FBI’s view that it was the largest crypto heist in history. The gap between the amount stolen and the amount frozen is substantial: more than $2.7 billion has been frozen so far, with court authority covering more than $1.2 billion of that total.

Freezes and seizure authority are different steps

The distinction matters because a freeze can prevent assets from moving while their ownership and ultimate disposition remain unresolved. In this case, U.S. agencies worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the proceeds. The Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain part of the frozen amount. That legal authority gives investigators a route toward forfeiture, but it is not the same as a final forfeiture order.

As the Associated Press reported, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That process adds a legal test after the technical work of locating and immobilizing assets. The faster route—coordinating with platforms to freeze suspected proceeds—can limit how much is moved beyond reach. The trade-off is that frozen funds are not yet a final recovery, and court proceedings still have to determine their disposition.

The remaining funds present a further challenge. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue laundering the assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those routes can complicate tracing and create opportunities for assets to move across services and networks. The FBI has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses the group is using to launder the Binance proceeds.

Tracing and forfeiture remain ahead

The next measures to watch are whether authorities and service providers can freeze more of the stolen assets, how much the government ultimately pursues through forfeiture, and what a judge rules on claims to the recovered funds. The current figures mark two different points in that process: more than $2.7 billion frozen through joint public and private efforts, and more than $1.2 billion covered by court authorization to seize or restrain. The Justice Department has not said that the full theft has been recovered, and its announcement says tracing and enforcement efforts are ongoing.
