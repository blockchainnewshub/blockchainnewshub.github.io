Binance confirms $6.3B hot-wallet loss, pledges full cover

Binance says hot wallets lost about $6.3 billion in tokens, promises to cover the loss and keeps withdrawals paused during security checks and recovery.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 30, 2026 · 21:09 UTC#064c404 min read

Binance has confirmed that attackers compromised its hot wallets and took about $6.3 billion in token assets, turning Thursday’s report of a “potential security issue” into a disclosed loss at the world’s largest crypto exchange. The exchange says it will cover the stolen funds, while withdrawals remain paused for security work; deposits and other unaffected services are operating. The Binance incident update puts the compromise at about 20:05 UTC on Oct. 29. The Associated Press reported that the exchange had earlier described an investigation and estimated impact, without explicitly confirming stolen funds.

The change matters because the scale is now paired with a concrete response: Binance says it has set aside more than $6.34 billion, including $5 billion in the same token assets that were stolen. It says it will use its Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU) and corporate treasury to cover the loss, and is buying the remaining affected tokens through market and over-the-counter channels. Those are Binance’s stated plans; the final technical account and independent verification have not yet been published.

What does Binance say was compromised?

Binance says its hot wallets were compromised, causing the loss of approximately $6.3 billion in token assets. Its initial theory had pointed to a potentially compromised employee laptop and access to a service container, but the exchange now says investigators have found additional breach points and are still reconstructing the attack. It has found no evidence that wallet private keys were leaked. That distinction narrows what Binance has confirmed about the route into its systems; it does not resolve how the attacker reached the wallets or whether other controls failed.

The exchange says about $3 billion has been frozen on-chain through work with law enforcement, blockchain intelligence firms, asset issuers and industry partners. The freeze is a substantial recovery effort, but it is not the same as recovering the full amount: Binance says it is separately preparing to replace the loss with its own funds. The AP reported that stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze assets linked to the incident, illustrating how issuer controls can help restrict some tokens after transfers. That response depends on assets being traceable and issuers able to act; it does not apply equally to every token or guarantee recovery.

What do customers gain and give up during the response?

Binance’s pledge to cover the loss shifts the immediate stated financial burden away from users and its reserve of matching tokens could help it make good on affected balances without first waiting for frozen assets to return. The company says this amounts to roughly 80% of the impacted assets already covered. The trade-off is that customers cannot withdraw crypto while the exchange checks and rebuilds the affected systems. Binance says deposits and other unaffected services remain operational, but that does not restore access to withdrawals.

That distinction separates exchange custody from holding assets in a wallet controlled by the user: Binance can pause platform withdrawals and coordinate freezes with issuers, while those same platform controls leave customers dependent on its recovery process. The immediate protection is a promise of reimbursement; the immediate cost is restricted access. Binance says it plans to resume withdrawals in phases, subject to security checks, and targets a start within about 34 hours of its Oct. 30 update. That is a target, not a confirmed reopening.

Which updates will show whether the response holds?

The next tests are whether phased withdrawals begin on schedule, whether Binance completes its token purchases and whether the promised proof-of-reserves snapshot supports its coverage claim. The company says it expects an initial snapshot within 12 hours and results by Monday, Nov. 2. A verified technical post-mortem, also promised after further investigation, should clarify how the additional breach points connected to the hot wallets. Until those details arrive, the $6.3 billion loss and Binance’s coverage commitment are clear; the full attack path, final recovery total and timetable for normal withdrawals remain unresolved.
