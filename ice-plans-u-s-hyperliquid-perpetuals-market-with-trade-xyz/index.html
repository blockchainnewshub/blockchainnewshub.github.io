ICE plans U.S. Hyperliquid perpetuals market with Trade[XYZ]

ICE and Trade[XYZ] plan a separate U.S. venue for Hyperliquid-based perpetuals, adding 24/7 retail exposure across assets if regulators approve the market.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomOct 30, 2026 · 20:01 UTC#5c63073 min read

Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the New York Stock Exchange’s parent, plans to operate a separate U.S. market for Trade[XYZ]’s Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures, a move that would bring round-the-clock derivatives into a regulated framework if approvals are granted. The companies announced the agreement on Oct. 29, saying the new venue would have its own market and clearing structure. ICE’s announcement says it will invest strategically in Trade[XYZ] and serve as the exclusive operator of its regulated markets.

The shift is from Trade[XYZ]’s existing Hyperliquid markets, which serve eligible users outside the U.S., to a planned U.S. structure with customer checks and separate clearing. ICE says the new retail business would sit apart from its institutional futures franchise. The announcement does not give a launch date, product list, or investment terms.

How would the U.S. market differ from Trade[XYZ]’s current venue?

It would put ICE’s exchange and clearing operation around markets that currently run on Hyperliquid’s infrastructure. Trade[XYZ] designs and implements the products, while Hyperliquid provides the underlying infrastructure, according to ICE. The companies intend to seek approvals for a dedicated market and clearing structure, including approvals needed to support leveraged trading.

ICE expects customers to access the regulated contracts through futures commission merchants that join the platform, after completing know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering checks. Distribution is expected through partners, brokers and platforms traders already use. ICE says its joint venture with OKX could be one route, subject to separate approvals for the venture’s U.S. broker-dealer and futures commission merchant subsidiaries.

Trade[XYZ]’s existing markets will continue alongside the proposed U.S. venue, according to CoinDesk’s report. That keeps the existing offshore operation distinct from the planned regulated market; the announcement does not say that the current contracts themselves will become available to U.S. customers.

What does the plan offer that traditional futures do not?

The proposed contracts would provide around-the-clock exposure to crypto, equities, equity indices, commodities and foreign exchange. Perpetual futures do not expire, unlike traditional futures contracts with set dates. ICE describes them as suited to short-duration exposure and retail trading, while its existing institutional franchise serves commercial hedgers managing risk over longer periods through traditional futures, forward-curve price discovery and centrally cleared settlement.

That separation is the core trade-off in ICE’s plan: perpetuals offer continuous access and a wider retail reach, while the established futures business retains its dated contracts and institutional focus. ICE says the dedicated structure would also mean its commercial hedgers and institutional customers bear no exposure to trading or counterparty risk on the new retail platform. The companies have not disclosed specific contract terms or how the clearing arrangement will work in practice.

What still needs to happen before U.S. traders can use it?

Regulatory approvals remain the main condition. ICE and Trade[XYZ] say they intend to seek them; their announcement does not say approval has been granted or identify a filing. The ICE-OKX distribution route has its own pending approvals, so a regulatory green light for the market alone would not settle how customers reach it.

Trade[XYZ] says its Hyperliquid markets have handled more than $650 billion in volume in just over a year, and CoinDesk reports the existing markets will keep running in parallel. That activity gives the proposed venue an established product base, but does not establish how much demand will transfer to a U.S. regulated market. The next signals are the approval process, the contracts and eligibility rules the companies disclose, and whether broker and platform partners join the venue.
