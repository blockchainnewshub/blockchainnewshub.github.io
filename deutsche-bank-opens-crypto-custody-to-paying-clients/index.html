Deutsche Bank opens crypto custody to paying clients

Deutsche Bank opened custody to paying clients and signed an exploratory asset-management pact, extending bank-led crypto services while keeping the offer institutional.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 12, 2026 · 02:31 UTC#aea1d43 min read

Deutsche Bank’s digital asset custody service went live for paying clients on November 11, moving the bank from a September plan subject to regulatory approvals to an operating service for institutional and corporate customers. Reuters reported the launch and the new memorandum with Deutsche Asset Management. The move gives eligible clients a bank that manages their wallets and private keys; it does not make the service a retail route into crypto or remove the assets’ market risks.

The service is aimed at Deutsche Bank’s corporate and institutional clients in Europe. In its September announcement, the bank said it would support Bitcoin, Ether and selected stablecoins or e-money tokens, including USDC, EURC and EURAU. The Block reported that the live service supports those five assets and lets clients transfer them to other parties. The bank manages the wallets and keys, reducing the need for clients to build custody infrastructure themselves.

What changes when a bank holds the keys?

For institutions, the offer trades direct control over private keys for custody through a regulated bank. Deutsche Bank’s September description says the service uses hardware protection for keys, separate warm and cold storage environments, divided duties and multi-person transaction approvals. These controls change how custody is operated; they do not protect clients from price declines, fraud or the failure of other market participants. The bank also said crypto-assets do not have a deposit-guarantee scheme comparable to eligible bank deposits.

That makes the service distinct from holding assets through a crypto-native custodian or maintaining an institution’s own custody setup, though the available reporting does not establish a direct comparison of fees, insurance or technical performance. Deutsche Bank’s model may reduce the operational burden of managing keys internally, while placing control and transfer processes with the bank. It is also narrower than a retail exchange account: the bank’s September announcement said the service was intended for eligible corporate and institutional clients, not retail customers.

What does the agreement with Deutsche Asset Management cover?

The two firms signed a memorandum of understanding to explore digital-asset collaboration, rather than announce a product launch. The Block reported that the work could cover tokenized money market funds, crypto-asset exchange-traded commodities and stablecoins, with custody, settlement, liquidity management, treasury and distribution among the areas under consideration.

The arrangement connects custody to possible investment products. Deutsche Asset Management already offers crypto-asset ETCs, according to The Block, and its chief product officer, Manfred Bauer, said the firm planned to launch a tokenized money market fund. But the MoU does not confirm when such a fund will launch, what it would hold or how it would be distributed. The Block reported Deutsche Asset Management had €1.22 trillion in assets under management as of September 30, giving the potential collaboration a large established asset-management base.

For now, the concrete change is custody for paying institutional clients; the broader product plans remain exploratory. Deutsche Bank’s stated medium-term aim is an integrated service for custody, tokenization and settlement, Reuters reported. The next signals are whether the bank broadens the supported assets or geography, and whether the asset manager turns its tokenized-fund plans into a specific product with confirmed terms and a launch date.
