US secures court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

US authorities have court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in stolen Binance crypto, within a wider freeze exceeding $2.7 billion.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 15, 2026 · 03:53 UTC#21d4993 min read

US authorities have secured court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a significant step beyond the broader freeze of more than $2.7 billion but short of resolving who will ultimately receive the funds. The Justice Department announced the action on November 12, weeks after the theft of more than $6 billion; the department says tracing, freezing and forfeiture work continues.

The Justice Department’s announcement says the $1.2 billion is part of the amount frozen through joint US government and private-sector efforts. The distinction matters: court authorization to seize or restrain assets does not itself establish that the money has been forfeited or returned to Binance or its customers. The department did not identify the specific assets or describe how the authorized restraint would be carried out.

From a billion-dollar theft to a legal claim

The FBI says members of the North Korean military group it calls TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The breach’s scale makes the $2.7 billion freeze a major interruption, but it leaves a substantial amount outside the total publicly reported as frozen. The department described the combined freeze as more than $2.7 billion, while the court-authorized seizure or restraint covers more than $1.2 billion of that amount.

According to the Justice Department, several federal units and law-enforcement partners worked with virtual-asset service providers to interdict the funds. That cooperation offers a faster route to stopping assets held or routed through services able to act on law-enforcement requests. But the same announcement says TraderTraitor actors continue laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that efforts to trace and freeze the assets remain underway.

The Associated Press report notes that the US government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That process is a different stage from freezing assets: it can establish legal control, but it also leaves questions about ownership and distribution to be resolved.

The distinction also shapes what the headline number can tell customers and markets. A freeze can prevent movement; seizure or restraint adds court-backed authority over assets; forfeiture would be a further legal step. None of those figures, on their own, confirms how much will be returned to affected users or when. The announcement gives no timetable for a final forfeiture outcome.

What remains to be established

The Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizing and forfeiting the stolen assets are ongoing. It also says the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating recovery efforts, and that a cyber alert issued the previous week urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions linked to addresses used to launder the Binance funds.

The next signals are whether authorities can convert the restraint into forfeiture, how much of the wider frozen total is ultimately recovered, and whether investigators can keep identifying assets as they move across services and protocols. For now, the $1.2 billion marks a legal foothold within a larger effort—not a confirmed payout.
