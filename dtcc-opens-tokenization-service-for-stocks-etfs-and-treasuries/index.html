DTCC opens tokenization service for stocks, ETFs and Treasuries

DTCC has opened a service for tokenized stocks, ETFs and Treasuries, bringing securities held in its custody onto blockchains while limiting access to approved firms and assets.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 3, 2026 · 15:43 UTC#b0303e4 min read

The Depository Trust Company, a DTCC subsidiary, launched its tokenization service on Oct. 28, bringing selected U.S. stocks, ETFs and Treasuries onto blockchain networks while keeping them within the existing custody system. DTCC’s launch announcement says the tokens represent securities held at DTC and retain their ownership rights, investor protections and economic entitlements. CoinDesk reports that the 34 securities eligible at launch represent about $30 trillion in market value; that is the eligible pool, not a reported amount already tokenized.

The shift is from planned rollout to a live service, after DTCC said it completed limited-production trades with more than 30 firms in July. It creates a route for moving traditional securities into digital wallets through core market infrastructure. That differs from synthetic stock tokens, which may track a share price without representing ownership of the underlying security, and from a stand-alone tokenized asset that sits outside DTC custody.

What does the $30 trillion launch cover?

It covers a defined set of eligible securities, not every asset DTC holds. According to CoinDesk’s report on the launch, the initial set includes selected Russell 1000 stocks, major-index ETFs and U.S. Treasury bills, notes and bonds. DTC has more than $114 trillion in assets under custody, so the launch universe is a subset of the securities held in its depository.

The service mints digital representations of DTC-custodied securities. Eligible users can convert securities between traditional and tokenized forms and deliver the tokens to registered digital wallets. The networks connected at launch are Canton Network and DTCC’s private Besu network. DTCC says additional networks are planned for 2027.

Twenty DTC participants have signed agreements to access the service, including J.P. Morgan, Citi, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Fidelity Investments and UBS. Signing up indicates access, not that each firm has already tokenized assets or completed transactions on the service. DTCC has not announced a tokenization volume in its launch release.

How does this compare with existing securities markets?

The main change is that blockchain-based transfers can now use digital representations of securities held within DTC’s established custody framework. DTCC says the tokens preserve the rights and protections attached to the traditional securities. CoinDesk reports they can move between registered wallets around the clock, while DTC retains the official ownership records.

That structure offers continuity with existing custody and oversight, alongside the potential for quicker transfers between wallets. But it also keeps participation within a defined institutional system: DTC participants and their clients use the service, the eligible assets are limited, and wallet transfers are subject to compliance restrictions. This is not an open-market tokenization of all U.S. securities, nor does a blockchain transfer by itself replace the established processes for trading and settlement.

The comparison is also with other market operators developing tokenized-securities infrastructure. CoinDesk reports that Nasdaq and the New York Stock Exchange are working on such systems, while DTCC’s approach centers on representing assets already held at DTC. The distinction is the connection to the existing depository; the trade-off is that access remains tied to its participants, approved assets and registered wallets.

What will show whether the service gains traction?

The next signals are actual usage, expansion of eligible securities and networks, and whether tokenized assets become useful in transactions beyond transfers. DTCC’s July production event included Treasury and equity trades, collateral pledges and delivery-versus-payment transactions, but those demonstrations do not establish routine volumes after launch. The company has said more participants will be onboarded over time and additional networks will follow in 2027.

For now, the launch puts tokenized securities closer to the center of U.S. market infrastructure, while leaving the size of real demand unproven. Watch for disclosed transaction activity, new assets and networks, and evidence that firms use the tokens to move collateral or settle trades—not only to create digital representations.
