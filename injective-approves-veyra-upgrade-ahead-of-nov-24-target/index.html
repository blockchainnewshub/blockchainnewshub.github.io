Injective Approves Veyra Upgrade Ahead of Nov. 24 Target

Injective approved Veyra with 99.51% support among non-abstaining votes, targeting a block-triggered Nov. 24 switch to software version 1.21.0.

By Crypto Docket NewsroomNov 22, 2026 · 07:30 UTC#8b6b473 min read

Injective voters approved the Veyra mainnet upgrade, moving the network from Meridian’s earlier groundwork toward more cross-chain asset transfers and institutional trading tools; activation is targeted for Nov. 24 at a specified block, not a fixed time. The final tally reported by The Defiant puts support at 99.51% of non-abstaining voting power, while the proposal’s governance record identifies the change as version 1.21.0.

The distinction between the vote’s headline approval rate and its full tally matters. Yes votes totaled about 16.24 million INJ in voting power, against roughly 80,589 no votes; about 7.75 million INJ abstained. Abstentions count toward participation but not the non-abstaining share used for the 99.51% figure. The Defiant reports turnout of 42.69%, above the 33.4% quorum threshold. The result clears the governance hurdle, but it does not mean 99.51% of individual stakers voted yes: the tally is weighted by voting power.

What Veyra adds to the existing system

The approved proposal describes new Inter-Blockchain Communication (IBC) capabilities for moving assets between Injective’s Ethereum Virtual Machine environment and the wider interchain ecosystem. The practical change is a route for connected assets to reach Injective’s environment and liquidity. It provides infrastructure for potential tokenized-asset activity; it is not itself an issuance of new assets.

Veyra also adds request-for-quote (RFQ) infrastructure and expands atomic-swap functionality. RFQ lets a participant request a price for a trade, while atomic swaps are designed to complete linked exchanges together or not at all. These tools extend Injective’s existing trading stack. The Defiant notes that Ethereum-compatible applications can already quote and execute spot swaps against Injective’s orderbook, so the proposal adds capabilities around trading and settlement rather than replacing that existing route.

That marks a different step from Meridian, which the proposal describes as the foundation for Veyra’s privacy work. The upgrade builds toward CypherOS, Injective’s planned native privacy platform, but does not launch CypherOS itself. The approved features focus on connectivity and trading infrastructure; confidential trading remains a future part of the roadmap. That sequencing gives developers and institutions new tools to build on while leaving privacy functionality for a later stage.

The block height sets the next test

The upgrade plan calls for the chain to halt at block 193,145,000 and resume on v1.21.0. Nov. 24 is the target for reaching that height, not a promised clock-time appointment. Injective’s v1.21.0 release was posted on GitHub on Nov. 17, giving node operators a release to prepare ahead of the scheduled halt.

For users and applications, the immediate test is whether the network reaches the upgrade height and resumes normally on the new version. For operators, the switch depends on installing compatible software and coordinating the restart. The proposal establishes the vote outcome and technical plan; it does not establish that every planned institutional workflow or asset market will arrive with the upgrade.

Next, watch for the chain’s progress toward block 193,145,000, confirmation that validators have resumed on v1.21.0, and follow-up details on how the IBC, RFQ and atomic-swap features are made available to applications. Those milestones will show how much of Veyra’s infrastructure becomes usable at activation, while CypherOS remains a separate future signal.
